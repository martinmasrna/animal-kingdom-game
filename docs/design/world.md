# The world

The game's animals live in a world of our own, not on Earth: decided by Martin on 2026-11-19 ("I thought about it and it's 1000% the right decision. It solves so many problems at once, not just art and card design"), after the research in `design/screen/hex/art/research/theme-and-style-2026-11-19.md`. Everything else on this page is his thinking aloud on 2026-11-19, recorded as reactions, not decisions. The method study from before the decision is `knowledge/world-first-study-2026-10-24.md` (written for real animals; its looks-come-free-from-the-species parts no longer hold). The societies already drafted are in `societies/`.

## His four answers, 2026-11-19

- **The one idea:** "No idea yet. Maybe we'll discover that." Candidates he floated and doubted: nature is beautiful, evolution ("I don't want to turn it into fucking Pokemon").
- **How human:** "human in personality, but not in appearance at all"; the creatures are invented ("we're not going to have a lion in our game. It's going to be some different animal that is just inspired by lion ... we need to fucking invent like actual animals"), each clearly resembling a real animal so people's built-in associations work for us ("we should in fact like fully lean into all the like already pre-sold notions that people have for animals"), but not copying it ("I wouldn't have like an actual ... tiger ... with like stripes"; his model: an elephant with four tusks, "subconsciously you still read elephant, but at the same time it's clearly comes from like a different world").
- **Tone:** the hexagonal tiles set the art to simple, close to mobile-game style, and he leans mobile-first ("if something does and looks well on mobile, it's going to do and look well on PC as well, but the other way around definitely not"). Not serious adult fantasy, not comedy like Exploding Kittens, Unstable Unicorns or Plants vs Zombies ("it doesn't really fit my style ... and also it doesn't really fit collectible card game crowd"). "celebrating like the beauty of nature, and like diversity" and "celebrating human creativity and like just creating your own worlds ... for the pleasure of it"; "playful ... not ... forced or heavy or dark"; "pure joy and like love for the world and just life itself", the world of a child before ego, status and other people's expectations.
- **Who lives in it:** societies, each with its own look ("the same way that elves have pointy ears"), silhouette and colours, and its own playstyle ("it's almost like you get to create like colors from magic without having to strictly define them in rules, and you get to do as many of them as you want"; snakes removal, birds control or card draw).

## His method ideas

- A society takes two well-known archetypes and combines them, surface from one and behaviour from the other, or both from both. The hoofed animals: elvish look and names, at peace with nature, pacifist, and nomadic because they follow the grass; a herd, less about individuals. The cats: orcs on the surface (simple, high-strength cards, the fighter; it fits Gorak One-Eye's name), but with strong family ties, individualist, in small packs.
- Contrast and tension inside every society, never one-dimensional: "whatever we choose should make sense".
- Free comprehension kept everywhere, not only in abilities: the look, the world, the story.
- Archetypes, never stereotypes ("people just kind of gloss over them and they mentally file them away"); "you take something familiar and then you put a twist on it. And it leaves a bit of a gap". Dragons not as beasts or wise elders; Le Guin's Earthsea dragons as an example of a twist; dragons as old gods floated, "a bit stereotypical" unless twisted.
- Habitats become regions of the world, as Riot's Freljord is the Arctic; expansions were planned per habitat. Open: whether societies follow the kind of animal (all cats) or the region (all Arctic animals). His leaning: the kind of animal is the race, a race can span regions, societies tie to regions, with culture and language on the society.
- "we're gonna have to do this like very, very slowly and gradually and methodically", with real research on worldbuilding first ("I don't want to jump into this only based on like your instruction based off your memory and like pre-trained weights").
- Experience: inventing worlds as a kid, small games with some worldbuilding, no formal education.

## What the inhabitants are, 2026-11-19

- His reactions: tools and armour make sense for thinking creatures, so the old no-human-made-things rule was a belief from the real-animal days; "no matter what we do, it shouldn't be humans that look like animals" (human bodies with animal heads read as furry, "boring, cheap, doesn't really fit my style"); Ice Age's characters read as animals with human personalities, not humans; they need human-like emotions to be relatable ("the game is fucking made by human for humans"). His generator question: what if every animal, not only one line of apes, had become as intelligent as humans (and then no humans would exist). His thread: animals have no ego the way humans do, and both self-expression and needless war come from ego.

## Invented creatures, 2026-11-19

- His thinking aloud, not yet decided: "the invented ones are going to work much better, but psychologically they need to clearly resemble the existing ones ... not ... some blobs that have no resemblance"; not Earth's animals made intelligent but "a planet where every living creature that exists kind of evolved with the human-like ego and self-awareness".
- On the art: invent two or three new creatures to see them, and push the tools and crafts "all the way" ("a monkey in a forge, like forging a sword").
