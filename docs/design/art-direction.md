# Art direction

## The feeling

Childlike wonder at the natural world. The feeling of being six or eight, reading animal books and staring at the pictures: a lion with a mane, a tiger with stripes, a rhino with a horn, a snake that has no legs at all, each one wildly different from the next. Then the second wave of the same wonder, later, on learning that none of it was designed: every strange feature was shaped by millions of years of evolution and exists for a reason. The diversity is natural, true to itself and unpretentious, and that is what makes it beautiful.

The art celebrates this. The test is the collection screen, the atlas of cards you own with the ones you don't yet own greyed out: someone opening it should stare at it in amazement.

## Principles

1. **Each card celebrates what makes that animal formidable or unique in nature, read through its card.** Start from the card's strength and effect and from what the animal *is* (the elephant: the most massive land animal, nothing dares attack it; strength 8, Immovable), not from its most famous body part in a pleasant scene (an elephant spraying water misses the card entirely).
2. **Diversity is the point.** The collection must read as a menagerie of wildly different creatures. One consistent rendering style, but silhouettes, poses, colours and compositions vary as much as the animals do; no single template pose or lighting recipe.
3. **True and unpretentious.** Real animals as they are, drawn so they're recognisable at a glance. No costumes, crowns or fantasy additions; nature's own design is enough. The rendering may be stylized.
4. **Clarity above cleverness.** No concept that makes the viewer decode the picture (double exposure, silhouette tricks).
5. **Emotion over medium.** Colour, proportion, composition and focus matter more than whether it's oil or gouache.
6. **Rarity shows through nature itself.** Commons are calm portraits of the whole animal in bright daylight in its habitat, the animal-book picture: the trait reads through pose and scale, not an action scene. Legendaries are specific, mythic individuals of a real species: nature rises around them and their own features are supercharged to mythical proportions (Methuselah's shell grown into a mossy hill under an aurora, Theron's mane like fire under a blood-red sun). They are supernormal in their own defining trait, not simply bigger (Methuselah's age shown as the patriarch among generations of descendants; Aquila's talons in the strike). Each legendary has its own light and palette; a shared stormy sky turns them into a template (the storm belongs to the Lion family). They may be dark and dramatic; commons may not. Never ornaments, crowns or costumes.
7. **The animal separates from its background.** Strong contrast of light and colour between the animal and what is behind it, and a simpler, softer background (Borealis: a white bear against a dark aurora sky). An ochre lion on ochre rock under an orange sky reads only once decoded.
8. **No template pose.** Each animal gets a characteristic pose of its own, chosen from its essence; a tiger that stands like the lion reads as a recoloured lion.

## Evaluate on the collection, not on one card

Art is judged as a grid of many different animals, some greyed out as not yet owned. A style that works for one lion can fail the collection by making everything look alike.

## What has been learned

Kept so far (generated, by look): hand-painted gouache in a bright palette (turquoise sky #51bcd0/#7ec3ca, warm ochres #cfab66/#c38f4a/#9b6d37, olive greens #9d9a4e/#6a7948, deep brown #58432e), bold oil, a 1930s travel-poster lithograph, and cave painting. Rejected: photographs (documentary, not a game), cute or chibi, dark and desaturated (every card looks the same), anime, pixel art, flat vector, woodblock, claymation, clever concepts, and flat or hand-coded SVG illustration. The card frame and composition around the art are unsolved; the frames tried so far were disliked.

The image model paints only the animal and its world. Every frame, piece of text and UI element is designed by hand. Cards carry only name, effect, strength and tag.
