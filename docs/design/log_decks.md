# Log: deck calls

Martin's calls made while a deck was being designed, dated, in his words. A log, not a plan: a deck is its list in `decklists.json`, and the reason behind a card's number is the note on that value in the card table (`tools/cards/hq.py show ID`).

- A card that only punishes one deck is bad design (the Anteater as an anti-Colony card, Giants, 2026-10-08).
- Giants, 2026-10-27. The inspiration is Ramp Druid and green ramp in Magic. Extra actions are no answer: "before you propose cards that add more action, that's not a solution". The Titans stay, and the deck needs ways to "cheat out" the Titan and Hungry giants, as the Chameleon does; the Blue Whale stays exactly as it is.
- Rejected that day, in his words: dropping Titan from the deck ("where exactly are you supposed to play those cards then, what's the point??"); Titans that can't be removed ("it's the same as just bolting Armor on top of all Titan cards"); a Titan filling two crossroads ("I can't imagine how we'd ever implement this in the engine, let alone visually"); a Blue Whale removing every small enemy ("doesn't fit the game, it's a hearthstone/magic card"); a common Chameleon ("too strong and too generic to be a common"); the Anteater removing a small adjacent enemy ("basically just remove the card from existence"); an Oxpecker whose neighbours don't eat ("The dungbeetle is supposed to be fuel for the hungry animal, not win condition"); the legendary Oxpecker gaining food from its Hungry neighbour ("not a legendary-worthy effect"), keeping its neighbours from losing strength ("helps against exactly 2 out of 200 cards"), and playing a Hungry animal next to itself ("too on the nose").
- Four legendary Oxpeckers on "synergy with big animals" (the host's strength, a giant that roams, a removed giant back to hand, covering next to a giant): "bad fit, and all clunky as well". On the legendary Dung Beetle's draw: "not sure if the legendary dung beetle is even legendary-worthy, it's just a boring draw engine just for this deck specifically".
- Primates (Handlock), 2026-10-27, a legendary Gorilla that grows on the board for each buffed animal in hand: "when are you going to understand that gaining strength while a unit is already on board is useless?"
- Giants, 2026-10-27, the legendary Oxpecker: "The effect should have nothing to do with removal. Also, it's fine if giant have a bad matchup against fast decks"; work from the proactive plan. On empty crossroads next to giants counting for regions: "the text might as well be complete gibberish", and "they don't 'win with regions'... They win by winning the board fight because their animals are bigger, that's about it."
- Kept that day: the Krill ("a bit on the nose but I guess it would help"), the Oxpecker's draw-and-food from the strongest animal in hand ("at strength 2 and flight"), the legendary Oxpecker's draw on a legendary Dung Beetle, the Anteater on the bench "we'll see which effects we need and pick it up then".
- Legendary pass, 2026-10-28, the Toad dropping Reach to become the plain Poison common: "you can't just drop a keyword from a card that is already not played by anyone and act like it's all fine". On the vanilla Armor common: the Stag Beetle is "already useless at 6"; the job is another Armor animal that fits a strength-6 body better, not a lower number.
