# The herds

The first society page, written 2026-10-24 to the form in `knowledge/world-first-study-2026-10-24.md` (section 6): observed nature written down, concept only, so the playstyle the family's cards serve, their effects, the art and the names read from one source. The cards themselves are not restated here: the table at the end is generated from `cards.json` and is always the current list of every card carrying the Hoofed tag. The herd is a family, not a deck: a player runs a few of these animals in a defensive or long-game deck beside other families; the Hoofed precon is one deck built from them to teach. Lines 1 to 4 are documentary and checkable; line 5 is the one invented thing and is a proposal for Martin. Nothing here is settled.

## 1. What they are

The grazers of the open plain: zebra, wildebeest, gazelle, with the giraffe above them, eating in the open all day in sight of everything that eats them. Nobody is in charge. The herd is safe because it is many, and it stays alive by seeing first and running together.

## 2. How they live

- Several species graze as one herd on purpose: zebra take the tall coarse grass, wildebeest the middle, gazelles the short new growth, so they share the ground instead of fighting over it; and each sees, hears or smells something the others miss.
- The others graze beside the giraffe. From up there it sees a lion a mile off; when a giraffe stops chewing and stares, the whole plain lifts its head.
- An alarm belongs to everyone. A wildebeest's snort, a zebra's bark, a gazelle's stamp, a baboon's cough: whoever gives it, every species runs.
- A gazelle that sees the cheetah bounces stiff-legged on the spot before it runs. It is telling the hunter it is fit; the hunter picks someone else.
- The one that is cut off dies. Predators do not fight a herd, they split one animal from it, so the herd bunches, and a running mass of zebra is hard to pick one from.
- The young are kept inside. A wildebeest calf stands in minutes and runs with the herd the same day; the calves are all born within a few weeks, more than the hunters can eat.
- They move with the rain. Zebra go first, wildebeest follow, gazelles last, on a route the old ones remember, and they cross the river where the crocodiles wait because the grass is on the other side.
- Cornered, they turn and kick. A giraffe's kick kills a lion; buffalo mob a lion and trample it; a zebra stallion drops to the rear when the harem runs and fights there; a warthog backs into its hole tusks first.
- They never stop eating. A grazer feeds most of the day and drinks every day or two, so the herd is wherever the grass and the water are, and it leaves the moment they run out.
- At night they bunch and stand. Zebra sleep standing and in turns; somebody is always awake.
- Oxpeckers ride on their backs, eat the ticks, and hiss when something creeps up.

## 3. The playstyle the herd's cards serve

Holding ground and winning the long game on food. A player who runs hoofed animals is choosing to hold regions with grazers rather than race or hunt: safe and productive together, broken when scattered, since one covered corner breaks the region and switches off every grazing bonus, and the fleers run home rather than die, keeping their Roar for the next placement. The more of them stand together, the more each is worth, so they reward a tight pasture and punish a deck that wins by covering; alone, a hoofed animal is a body that holds a corner and little more. The precon (Martin, 2026-10-08; `effects-pass.md`, Hoofed Graze) is the all-in version: both home regions held by grazers, Magic's Pillowfort on a piece of land.

## 4. Who does what

The roles the behaviours give a herd, each a kind of card the family can hold:

- **The eyes.** The one who sees first, and the wall when the hunters come at night.
- **The voice that gathers.** The one whose call brings the others in; worth more the more of the herd stands around it.
- **The memory of the route.** The one who knows where the grass is; the herd's food.
- **The runners.** The ones that flee and come back, and the calf that runs the day it is born.
- **The ones that turn and fight.** The mob, the stallion at the rear, the tusks in the hole: the herd's answer to what covers it.

Behaviours on line 2 that no role uses yet (the alarm that belongs to everyone, the calving that swamps the hunters, the river crossing, the oxpecker) are where a new card for the family starts; a concrete card idea goes into `cards.json` as a planned card with its note, and appears in the table below.

## 5. Who names whom (proposal)

A herd has no house and no birth-naming. A wildebeest calf is one of half a million born the same month, and nobody keeps a lineage, so herd animals do not carry kin names the way the Pride carries Theron and Adira (which is why the coined birth-names of 2026-10-21 had nothing under them). What a herd has is jobs: the one who sees first, the one whose bark everyone runs from, the one who remembers the river. So the herd names an individual by what the herd relies on it for, in the plain words a grazer has: sight, sound, light, weather, grass, water, the route, the river. Said with relief, the way the Giraffe legend has it.

The hunters have their own word for the same animals, said as a curse, and the two never match. Which word the card carries is open per legend (Martin, 2026-10-21: "we don't have to commit upfront").

A herd name is a call-name: one to three beats, firm, no drawing room, said across open ground. The anchors are the gold names of the same shape, Adira and Rue, not any herd name yet, since none exists.

Words the herd cannot know: anything made (no tool, rope, tower, wall, watchman, sentinel, lookout as a post), the sea, snow, the forest's trees, the names of the hunters' things. Titles: none; a herd has no rank. The old cow who knows the route is the nearest thing to one, and that is a job, not a title.

Goals for the Giraffe's name, in order, for the naming skill's pool: the word the herd says with relief when they see her; about seeing, height or the first light; a call-name of one to three beats; not the job itself as a word (no Lookout, Watcher, Tower); says nothing about lions.
