# Naming legendaries

A legendary's name is what the animals call that individual, rendered into English the way Tolkien renders Rohan as Old English and Adams renders Lapine. The animals naming it may be its own kin, its prey or its predators: a terror of the sea carries the name its prey gave it, an unkillable one the name its predators gave it. Commons and rares carry plain species names; only legendaries are named. Researched 2026-10-09 (Sanderson, Tolkien, The Lion King, The Jungle Book, Watership Down, Warriors, Riot, Magic), then refined by Martin's calls.

## The test

- **Half a second, both ways.** Hear the name and you see the card (its art first: who the animal is and how it looks); think of the card and the name comes back. Prince Leo and Princess Lea are two lion cubs, siblings; Greywhisker is an old grey rat; Black Swan is the black swan.
- **How would a cat know what a hammer is?** (Warriors renamed its villain Hammerclaw to Tigerclaw days before print.) Every word lives in the animal's world: body, colour, size, sound, food, place, weather, kin. No tools, machines, jobs or institutions: no Tank, Professor, Tower.
- **It names this individual, not the species.** Greywhisker is one old rat; Highneck would be every giraffe.
- **It doesn't promise a mechanic the card lacks** (Magic renamed Spirit Lynx to Spectral Lynx for that). The ability may show through when it's the animal's most striking trait.

## Where a name comes from

The namers are the animals, never people (Martin, 2026-10-14: "the names need to be names the animals would have given them, not humans"). How a name gets made is the same as for real named animals (researched 2026-10-14): someone saw one concrete thing about this individual and said it. Here the someone is the forest, the pride, the river: a mark the others see (Greywhisker; Watership Down's Bigwig is Thlayli, "fur-head"), something they lived through with it, or a likeness to something in their own world.

- **Only words from the animals' world:** body, colour, smell, sound, weather, plants, places, the other animals. Never a human thing or human role: no Domino, Disco, Lipstick, Sultan, Bandit, Grandpa, Tank, Drum.
- **Name the scar, not the superpower.** A name built backwards from a concept (it stinks, so Brimstone; it hoards, so Pinch) is a pun and sounds artificial (Martin, 2026-10-14: "it sounds so artificial, all these names").
- **The meaning has to reach a player who speaks English.** A foreign word works only when players already know it (Leo is lion to everyone); a Swahili, Zulu or Latin word whose meaning only a dictionary knows is a coined sound (Ingwe, Tamias, Kifaru were "unpronounceable gibberish", Martin, 2026-10-14).
- **A legendary name belongs to one animal.** A name any animal could carry (Little One, Speedy, Shaggy) is no name for a legend.
- **Every name is built its own way.** A set made from one template (Lord or Lady on everything, an adjective plus a body part on everything) is the J.K. Rowling failure, Professor Sprout for the herbology teacher. Variety of source is part of what reads as real.
- **The name's weight matches the card's strength and role.** Ghost is a perfect name for a strength-8 apex predator that roams and strikes from range, and a lie on a strength-1 squirrel; Goliath promises a 9 or 10, not a strength-4 gorilla that buffs the hand (Martin, 2026-10-14). Read the strength and the effect before naming, not only the animal and the art.
- **The effect tells you the character.** An animal that draws several cards is one of the clever ones (raven, fox, owl): the Macaque legendary is a cunning crime boss who knows how to steal the food, not a greedy glutton (Martin, 2026-10-16: "I see him almost as like a crime boss... very cunning and clever").
- **The sound carries the character; meaning is optional.** Ragnaros sounds huge and burning, Yogg-Saron wrong and ancient, Geralt gruff and worn, before anyone knows what they mean (Martin, 2026-10-16: "why does there have to be perfect hidden meaning in some forgotten language??"). Choose names for how they sound against the animal, its art, strength and role. A meaning underneath (Luna, Yuki, Rue) is a bonus, never the search. A coined sound fails only when it evokes nothing (Tocca, Rasca); a coined sound that sounds like the character is a name.
- **It reads as a name, not a thing.** Moss, Fern and Mist sound like the thing, a description, not a name. Lengths vary: settling into one- and two-syllable names makes every legendary blur into the next (Martin, 2026-10-14 and 2026-10-16).
- **No verbs as names** (Lull, Pinch), and no single object the animal is known for (Drum for a gorilla is Acorn for a squirrel).
- **A name fits its animal, not only its own sound.** Ghost is close to a perfect name, but it belongs on a white predator, not a white squirrel (Martin, 2026-10-14).
- **Name one card at a time and get it right,** never a page of fifty (Martin, 2026-10-14). Only the six launch habitats are named now.

## Pronounceable by default

A player must be able to say the name. A foreign word is fine when it reads easily (Leo, Twiga, Zuri, Cuco); a hard one (Hrafn, Nyumbu, Paraoa) is allowed for at most about one legendary in ten, and only when it is so distinct it sticks anyway, the way Yggdrasil does. Distinct beats exotic: an unusual name that is merely unfamiliar (Chui) is forgettable.

## Picture first

Name a legendary after its art is settled, not before: the name is easiest to find once the picture exists and already matches the animal, the ability and the strength (Riot names champions the same way, after art and personality).

## What fails

- Everyday Western given names (Magnus, Clara, Martin): they read as a person, not an animal.
- Abstract nouns and verbs (Ruin, Cache, Migration, Harvest): they read as card effects.
- Coined sounds that evoke nothing (Tocca, Rasca).
- Clever two-word mash-ups (Gravepicker, Drillmaster): the AI-startup reflex.
- Words with an established other meaning (Leviathan) and anything citing a work, character, god or real animal (Scrooge, Medusa, Moby Dick, Cecil).
- Defaulting to one trick: not every legendary is Old or Big.

## How a naming round runs

- One card per message, with at least ten options built from different angles and techniques, never four variations of one idea (Martin, 2026-10-14: "it doesn't cost you anything").
- No marked pick and no ranking: Martin reacts to the list unbiased (2026-10-14: "I cannot look at this unbiased").
- After a rejection, change the source of the names, not their extreme: the two failure modes are plain English nouns (Nettle, Moss) and dictionary foreign words for the trait (Ingwe, Stella), and swinging between them is the yo-yo Martin called out on the Manta (2026-10-14).
- A name goes into the card data (`hq.py set ID animal "Name/Species"`) the moment Martin settles it. Names held back for the end of a pass get lost.

## Gold

The names Martin is fully happy with, the only examples for new names (2026-10-16): Prince Leo and Princess Lea, King Theron, Queen Adira, Greywhisker, Black Swan, Big Gus, Lord Bumbleton, Aurum, Iris, Yuki, Pestis, Eon, Vesper, Lobo, Scarlett, Roach King. They are built every way there is (a title and a name, a mark, a nickname, a plain word, a borrowed word); there is no single pattern behind them, and naming takes taste and context, not a formula (Martin, 2026-10-16: "stop trying to find a single golden pattern").

## Settled

Prince Leo, Princess Lea, King Theron, Queen Adira (lions); Black Swan; Greywhisker (rat); Arne, whose mate token is Arna (eagle); Big Gus (capybara); Lord Bumbleton (bumblebee); Fathom (octopus: the deep, and to fathom); Rue (skunk: the bitter herb animals keep away from by smell, and "you'll rue the day"); Luna (white wolf: the moon she howls at, a word every player knows); Yuki (white squirrel: Japanese for snow, known from anime); Aya (sloth: from ai, the three-toed sloth's own cry); Calypso (jellyfish: "she who hides", the sea nymph who keeps whoever comes close; an accepted exception to the myth rule because the word now lives on its own); Iris (the rainbow octopus: Greek for rainbow, and the ring of the eye); Brutus (rhinoceros); Cuco (cuckoo); Redbeak (oxpecker: the first thing you see, the word everyone says); Stonetusk (boar: tusks that would break rock). Kept without a fresh call: Pestis, Ember, Aurum, Eon, Methuselah, Silverback, Queen Marabunta, Queen Honoria, Vesper, Lobo, Gale, Barley, Scrooge and Mocha (the last two cite a work and a real whale; kept knowingly). Questionable, to revisit: Scrooge, Methuselah, Calypso and Brutus, never examples for new names (Martin, 2026-10-16: "All of them are questionable, I'm not 100% happy with them"). Scarlett (fox) stays while Vixen is in doubt (Martin, 2026-10-16: "not sure about Vixen over Scarlet"). Mimi, Zuri, Twiga and Eber, settled on 2026-10-09, were reopened on 2026-10-14 as the dictionary-word construction that fails above; Cinder (leopard) was settled and unlocked the same day.
