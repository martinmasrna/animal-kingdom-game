# Naming legendaries

Commons and rares carry plain species names; only legendaries are named, and a legendary's name is what the animals call that individual, rendered into English. The process is the workspace's `naming` skill; why it is shaped that way is `../../knowledge/naming-diagnosis-2026-10-16.md`. Names waiting for a card are on the shelf, `../cards/name-shelf.md`. Which names are settled, draft or placeholders is the card table (`./cards`), never this file.

## The test

Hear the name inside an animal card game and you see the card, its art first; think of the card and the name comes back. Prince Leo and Princess Lea are two lion cubs, siblings; Greywhisker is an old grey rat; Black Swan is the black swan. It works the same for a non-native player, which is most of the audience.

## Gold

The names Martin is fully happy with, the only examples for new names (Martin, 2026-10-16): Prince Leo and Princess Lea, King Theron, Queen Adira, Greywhisker, Black Swan, Big Gus, Lord Bumbleton, Aurum, Iris, Yuki, Pestis, Eon, Vesper, Scarlett, Roach King. They are built every way there is: a title and a name, a mark, a nickname, a plain word, a borrowed word, a species name, a joke. There is no pattern behind them and no rule file reproduces them (a cold judge applying the old rules passed 6 of these 17). Eleven of them came with Martin's own deck design and were lived with for months before he called them gold; the rest were grabbed on sight from a wide list, or absorbed through play.

## What Martin has said is absolute

- A player must be able to say it. A hard name is allowed for about one legendary in ten, when it is so distinct it sticks anyway, the way Yggdrasil does.
- The words come from the animal's world. No tools, machines or materials people make: no Tank, Drum, Razorback, Chalk, Ivory. Titles (King, Queen, Lord) are the accepted exception.
- No name a famous character of the same kind already wears (no Akela for a wolf, no Sirius either).

Everything else is taste, and taste is Martin's: a name he grabs is a name, whatever shape it has.

## Placeholders

A legendary without a name carries its species in caps and brackets, `[WOLF]`, or `Queen [ANT]` where the title is settled. The brackets ship; the names come when they come, at about one or two per sitting, and a name locks only after a week on the card and one cold look.
