# Log: rarity calls

Martin's calls on which rarity a card sits at, dated. A log, not criteria: the rarity rules are the Rarity section of [`principles.md`](principles.md).

## 2026-10-08 and 2026-10-09

- A Hamster isn't a rare (2026-10-08).
- The Octopus is a rare by feel (2026-10-08).
- How common an animal is in the wild doesn't decide its rarity; how special it feels does, and only a real mismatch counts (a pet or a forgettable rodent on a premium card). The Fox stays rare: its buff-and-roam draw engine would be too much at three copies, and the clever fox of the fables carries a rare fine. The Giants deck already reads this way: Rhinoceros and Hippopotamus are rares, the Elephant and Blue Whale commons (2026-10-09).
- Rate fixes over rarity changes when the animal fits its rarity: the Whale Shark rare draws 1 at Dusk (2 was a free Draw action every turn), the Termite King rare draws 2 (its Queen condition is hard to meet), the Manta Ray legendary draws 2. The Flying Squirrel stays rare: Flight on the common Squirrel's effect is a real upgrade, and the only flying rodent is a rare animal (2026-10-09).
- When unsure between too weak and too strong, ship the strong version: a broken card is obvious in play and easy to nerf, a weak one just goes unplayed. The Zebra legendary counts every different Hoofed animal you control, not only adjacent ones (2026-10-09).
- A legendary that is a common's effect doubled with a keyword added is lazy, even at the right rate: rework it around something the animal is known for. Vesper became the guard that lands on whoever covers a Queen; Methuselah became the tortoise of the fable, capping each player's food per turn so the slow deck catches up. A legendary close to a rare of the same shape gets numbers that put clear space between them (the Leopard legendary at 8 over the Orca's 7) (2026-10-09).

## 2026-10-27

- No common plays another card. The Remora became a rare at 3 and the Fish starter runs two Great White Sharks again; the Alley Cat's extra play moves to the Ocelot rare, since an alley cat doesn't read as a rare, and the Alley Cat gets a new common effect.

## 2026-10-22, the pass by the three-copies test

The rarity question is what goes wrong if the opponent faces this card three times a game, never which slot its deck has free. Macaque, Jackal, Serval, Barracuda, Termite Queen, Remora, Nurse Bumblebee, Termite King and Praying Mantis became commons, their strength to re-level against their new peers; Boar and Chimpanzee became rares at 4. The Rat stays a common and pays for its any-size removal with Den Rush's own condition, Hare's three animals in a turn, instead of a random discard every deck pays alike. The Earthworm stays rare (its two Worms draw too, three cards in all, where the Cockroach draws none). The Tarsier stays rare and its owner picks the Primate the +2 lands on. The City Spider stays common. The Moose stays rare: the Oxpecker counts animals of 8 or more, so it stays an 8, and Hungry is a second keyword. The bomb that removes everything next to it when removed is a rare Dart Frog with Poison (only a removal clears it, and that sets it off); the Toad is the plain common poisoner. The Dormouse stays rare as Food Aggro's refill (Dusk: if you gained 10 or more food this turn, draw a card): a common's food engine with a bigger number would have been the Worker Wasp again, or a lock behind walls. The Mackerel went to the reserve rather than to rare: its +1 lasts only while a 2 stands, so the Mahi-mahi's permanent +1 on a bigger body did its job better, and Fish had two common designs more than it needed; the Fish starter runs three Barracudas and Remoras and one Great White Shark.
