# Rarity

The fourth stage of locking a card, after completeness, wording and the animal–effect fit. It judges whether each card sits at the right rarity, card by card. The starter decks are only precons: a card's rarity is right or wrong on its own, whatever its deck would need, and fixing a deck's counts comes after. Every judgment is a proposal with its reason; nothing changes until Martin agrees.

## Two readings that should agree

- **The role.** Commons (3 copies) are the consistent engine a deck runs on. Rares (2 copies) are the stronger or more situational cards: removal, reactive answers, tech, and upgrades that would be too much at three copies, where drawing one is a real risk and you play it carefully. Legendaries (1 copy) are unique, potentially deck-defining: a build-around (few of these), an amplifier that pushes a plan over the edge without changing it (King Theron, Queen Adira), or a generally excellent card good in many decks (Prince Leo and Princess Lea, Aurum).
- **The animal.** How rare the animal feels to a normal person. A Hamster reads wrong as a rare; a Chinchilla read right; the Octopus felt rare before its effect existed. A one-of-a-kind animal sits naturally at rare or legendary, a lookalike at common.

Ideally both readings agree. When they don't, the card is flagged, and the fix is whichever change makes them agree: the card's rarity, or the animal carrying it.

## The test

Copies set how often a card shows up, and its rate (strength, numbers) sets how strong it is. So the rarity question is what goes wrong if the opponent faces this card three times a game, never which slot its deck has free (Martin, 2026-10-22).

- **Common:** the deck's own verb, done once. Removal, draw and extra plays are commons when their condition is the deck's own engine (food gained, a Colony count, an ally removed, cards in hand, Canines next to the target): three copies are fine because the card works only in that deck and only while the deck is doing its thing (Muskrat, Soldier Ant, Piranha, Macaque, Jackal, Serval, Barracuda, Termite Queen, Remora).
- **Rare:** good because it's occasional. An answer that slots into any deck (removal gated by the target's strength or state, bounce, tech: Jaguar, Black Mamba, Hawk, Boar, Chimpanzee); a swing bigger than one for one (mass removal, an extra action, draw 3, a removed card that keeps paying like the Earthworm's three cards); an ongoing effect the board plays around (Leopard, Dhole, Cape Buffalo, Porcupine); a card too narrow to want three of.
- For removal the line is where the condition lives: on your deck, common; on the target, rare. Three copies of a generic answer in every deck would mean no wall ever holds.

Rares therefore hold most of the pool's generic answers and are shared across decks, the way every Magic deck runs some removal. A starter runs as many rare designs as its cards have, not a fixed four.

## Things that bear on the role

- Copies change reliability: a second copy doubles how often you see a card, a third adds half again. A game plan is never built on drawing a legendary unless the deck searches for it.
- A card that would be too strong at three copies sits higher; so does a card too narrow to want three of. Neither is a test on its own: some rares are rare because they are situational, others because three would break the game.
- A higher rarity gets a better rate, never a worse one: a rare or legendary doing a common's job is at least as strong.
- Legendaries amplify the plan rather than replace it, and are unique: never a reprint of another card, never a plain card draw or +1 anthem.

## Grades

- **Right:** role and animal both fit the rarity.
- **Too high / too low:** the role belongs at another rarity. Say which, and whether the animal still fits there.
- **Animal mismatch:** the role fits the rarity but the animal reads as another one. Propose a better animal for the slot (respecting the fit criteria in `animal-fit.md`).
- **Unclear role:** the card doesn't do any rarity's job well; say what's wrong.

## Calls so far

- The 2026-10-22 pass by the test: Macaque, Jackal, Serval, Barracuda, Termite Queen, Remora, Nurse Bumblebee, Termite King and Praying Mantis became commons, their strength to re-level against their new peers; Boar and Chimpanzee became rares at 4. The Rat stays a common and pays for its any-size removal with Den Rush's own condition, Hare's three animals in a turn, instead of a random discard every deck pays alike (Martin, 2026-10-22). The Earthworm stays rare (its two Worms draw too, three cards in all, where the Cockroach draws none). The Tarsier stays rare and its owner picks the Primate the +2 lands on. The Dormouse stays rare as Food Aggro's refill (Dusk: if you gained 10 or more food this turn, draw a card): a common's food engine with a bigger number would have been the Worker Wasp again, or a lock behind walls. (Martin, 2026-10-22). The Mackerel went to the reserve rather than to rare: its +1 lasts only while a 2 stands, so the Mahi-mahi's permanent +1 on a bigger body did its job better, and Fish had two common designs more than it needed; the Fish starter runs three Barracudas and Remoras and one Great White Shark (Martin, 2026-10-22).

- A Hamster isn't a rare (Martin, 2026-10-08).
- The Octopus is a rare by feel (Martin, 2026-10-08).
- How common an animal is in the wild doesn't decide its rarity; how special it feels does, and only a real mismatch counts (a pet or a forgettable rodent on a premium card). The Fox stays rare: its buff-and-roam draw engine would be too much at three copies, and the clever fox of the fables carries a rare fine. The Giants deck already reads this way: Rhinoceros and Hippopotamus are rares, the Elephant and Blue Whale commons (Martin, 2026-10-09).
- Rate fixes over rarity changes when the animal fits its rarity: the Whale Shark rare draws 1 at Dusk (2 was a free Draw action every turn), the Termite King rare draws 2 (its Queen condition is hard to meet), the Manta Ray legendary draws 2. The Flying Squirrel stays rare: Flight on the common Squirrel's effect is a real upgrade, and the only flying rodent is a rare animal (Martin, 2026-10-09).
- When unsure between too weak and too strong, ship the strong version: a broken card is obvious in play and easy to nerf, a weak one just goes unplayed. The Zebra legendary counts every different Hoofed animal you control, not only adjacent ones (Martin, 2026-10-09).
- A legendary that is a common's effect doubled with a keyword added is lazy, even at the right rate: rework it around something the animal is known for. Vesper became the guard that lands on whoever covers a Queen; Methuselah became the tortoise of the fable, capping each player's food per turn so the slow deck catches up. A legendary close to a rare of the same shape gets numbers that put clear space between them (the Leopard legendary at 8 over the Orca's 7) (Martin, 2026-10-09).
