# Rarity

The fourth stage of locking a card, after completeness, wording and the animal–effect fit. It judges whether each card sits at the right rarity, card by card. The starter decks are only precons: a card's rarity is right or wrong on its own, whatever its deck's 4 legendaries, 4 rares and 6 commons would need, and fixing a deck's counts comes after. Every judgment is a proposal with its reason; nothing changes until Martin agrees.

## Two readings that should agree

- **The role.** Commons (3 copies) are the consistent engine a deck runs on. Rares (2 copies) are the stronger or more situational cards: removal, reactive answers, tech, and upgrades that would be too much at three copies, where drawing one is a real risk and you play it carefully. Legendaries (1 copy) are unique, potentially deck-defining: a build-around (few of these), an amplifier that pushes a plan over the edge without changing it (King Theron, Queen Adira), or a generally excellent card good in many decks (Prince Leo and Princess Lea, Aurum).
- **The animal.** How rare the animal feels to a normal person. A Hamster reads wrong as a rare; a Chinchilla read right; the Octopus felt rare before its effect existed. A one-of-a-kind animal sits naturally at rare or legendary, a lookalike at common.

Ideally both readings agree. When they don't, the card is flagged, and the fix is whichever change makes them agree: the card's rarity, or the animal carrying it.

## Things that bear on the role

- Copies change reliability: a second copy doubles how often you see a card, a third adds half again. A game plan is never built on drawing a legendary unless the deck searches for it.
- A card that would be too strong at three copies sits higher; so does a card too narrow to want three of. Neither is a test on its own: some rares are rare because they are situational, others because three would break the game.
- A higher rarity gets a better rate, never a worse one: a rare or legendary doing a common's job is at least as strong.
- Legendaries amplify the plan rather than replace it, and are unique: never a reprint of another card, never a plain card draw or +1 anthem.

## Grades

- **Right:** role and animal both fit the rarity.
- **Too high / too low:** the role belongs at another rarity. Say which, and whether the animal still fits there.
- **Animal mismatch:** the role fits the rarity but the animal reads as another one. Propose a better animal for the slot (respecting the fit criteria in `animal-fit.md`).
- **Unclear role:** the card doesn't do any rarity's job well; say what's wrong.

## Calls so far

- A Hamster isn't a rare (Martin, 2026-10-08).
- The Octopus is a rare by feel (Martin, 2026-10-08).
- How common an animal is in the wild doesn't decide its rarity; how special it feels does, and only a real mismatch counts (a pet or a forgettable rodent on a premium card). The Fox stays rare: its buff-and-roam draw engine would be too much at three copies, and the clever fox of the fables carries a rare fine. The Giants deck already reads this way: Rhinoceros and Hippopotamus are rares, the Elephant and Blue Whale commons (Martin, 2026-10-09).
