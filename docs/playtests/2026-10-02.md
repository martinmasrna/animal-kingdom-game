# Playtest read, 2026-10-02

Every web game people have played, read back move by move: 393 games (429 human seats), tied to who played them. 183 older games no longer replay because cards changed since, so this is mostly 2026-10-01. Players: Laras (208 games), Martin (87), Kalista (87), two guests (3). Tutorial games are not logged.

How it was read: `animal_kingdom/web/playtest.py` rebuilds each game through the engine and flags facts (an Apex that didn't eat, a Roar that acted on nothing, a placed animal removed at once, a long think, a turn ended with moves left); Luna read each player's games in order (37 batches) for confusion and frustration; every claim below was then checked against the games themselves. Rerun: `deploy/deploy.sh pull && deploy/deploy.sh players && .venv/bin/python -m animal_kingdom.web.playtest`.

## Findings, most important first

### 1. Kalista, the closest thing to a typical new player, loses half her games to the Easy bot

Kalista went 16–18 against Easy, 14–19 against Normal and 4–11 against people, over 87 games in one day; Laras went 105–60 against Expert. Kalista is the player who wrote that Defeat felt aggressive: she sees it in more than half her games. Easy is GreedyBot, a bot that takes the best move it can see every time; for a player still learning the cards that is not easy. She kept playing, which says the game holds her; how long a run of mostly losses keeps a newcomer is the question. **Your call:** whether Easy should be weaker (a bot that sometimes takes a worse move, or a gentler deck), and whether the ladder should put a new player against it until they win some.

### 2. When the opponent's reaction removes your animal, nothing shows what did it

14 times a player's animal was removed the moment it landed by something on the other side: Hippopotamus (an enemy of 3 or less placed next to it), Hedgehog and Porcupine spines (the first enemy to cover them), Grizzly Bear's strike. On screen the animal drains away and nothing points at the Hippo or the Hedgehog. The engine records the cause of every event; the client never reads it. Luna read these as the game misbehaving ("disappeared immediately with no stated cause"), which is what a player sees too. **Your call:** whether the cause should show (the reacting animal answering in the same beat as the removal, as a fact of what happened, not a hint of what will).

### 3. Apex Predators land on Armor and silently don't eat

Kalista put Tiger on an Armadillo twice and on Methuselah once, and Eon on an Armadillo once; Martin put Tiger on an Armadillo once. Each time the predator only covered it, the same surprise as your Eon on Black Panther last night, with Armor instead of Stealth. Armor's line says "can't be eaten", so the rule is stated; the landing still looks like an eat that failed. **Your call:** keep it (Armor is armor), or let an Apex not land on an Armor animal at all, so the move that can't do what the card says isn't offered.

### 4. The first player's edge, measured on human games

People win 69% going first (189 games) and 55% going second (182). Most are against bots; between two people the sample is 46 seats, too small alone. `pre-launch.md` asked for this measurement before any rule change; this is the first one.

### 5. The turn clock's starting numbers fit

Between people the median decision takes 3.5 s; 1.5% go past the 30 s free window; a player's whole game takes 2 minutes at the median and 5 at the 90th percentile, so the 3-minute bank is touched only by the rare long think. Times include the animations before each move, so thinking is a little shorter still. Nothing to change.

### 6. Where newcomers stop is invisible

18 of 24 profiles never finished a match. Most were made in bursts minutes apart (12:01–12:25, 20:05–20:12), so many are likely the same people on another device or browser, but the data can't say, because the tutorial isn't logged: a player who quits in lesson 1 leaves no trace. **Your call:** record each profile's furthest tutorial step (invisible to players, a few lines on the server); I'd build it.

## Looked at and not a problem

- **Removal Roars played with no target** (Kalista's Caracal half the time, Jaguar a third): almost always onto a crossroad with no target at all, not onto an enemy, and Martin's games show the same; these are 5–6 strength bodies placed for the board, with the Roar as a bonus when it lands.
- **Hornet vanishing**: an error in the first pass of the script (the Hornet discarded from hand to pay for the Roar was read as the one placed). Hornet works as written.
- **Eon leaving at once** (14 plays): its card text, and people keep choosing it.
- **Long thinks and turns ended with moves left**: mostly Martin's games (a 43-minute pause is a tab left open) and empty hands.
- **Bird Eggs removed before they hatch** (Laras): play, not confusion.
