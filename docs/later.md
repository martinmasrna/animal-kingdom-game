# Later

Work parked on purpose. Each item says why it exists and what would make it the next thing, so it is picked up for the right reason and not before.

- **A different painter for card art (parked 2026-10-17).** The art diagnosis ([`../knowledge/card-art-diagnosis-2026-10-17.md`](../knowledge/card-art-diagnosis-2026-10-17.md)) found that words never moved the pictures, so the one lever left is what paints. Two ways: a second generator on the same lines (Flux, Imagen; cents a picture; judged blind against the current model), or a style add-on (a LoRA) trained on the forty locked paintings over an open model (about five dollars and an hour for one run; public galleries prove a hand stays locked across subjects and prove nothing about Martin's hand; he wants proof before spending, and the only proof is the run). Pick it up when `art.py cost` shows the lean card-art skill did not bring pictures per installed card below about 8 after four weeks, or when the alpha needs art faster than the image quota gives it.
- **The Open Ocean set's repeated look (Martin, 2026-10-17).** About half the Ocean cards share the same underwater picture: light shafts coming down from the middle of the surface. He wants something done about it eventually; new Ocean pictures avoid it from now, and the existing ones are a consistency pass for when the set is otherwise settled.
- **Few tactical moments (Martin and agent, 2026-11-11).** A puzzle session built from real games found few good puzzles: a strong player sees the best move at once, a weaker one misses it, and even shown it, the reason isn't plain. Reading of why (agent's, not settled): most turns force nothing, so the best move is a judgment about future turns and the hidden hand, not a calculation; chess-style "found it" moments need forcing threats and double threats, and here only the den and a food total near the line force a reply. The lever: more cards that keep acting on their neighbouring crossroads while they stand (traps, auras, zones that put a price on landing nearby, never forbid it: a frontier five spots wide freezes otherwise), and more threats that force a reply. Test: puzzles whose answer is beyond argument become easy to find in real games. Pick it up at the next card round that has room for new effects.
- **Randomness seen before the decision (Martin and agent, 2026-11-11).** Martin: random effects may have been thrown out more than needed. Garfield's distinction: randomness before a decision (choose one of three, a token or food that appears visibly a turn ahead) creates decisions and keeps positions fresh; randomness after a decision (did it work?) replaces them. A hypothesis, not a finding: the case is freshness, and weaker players winning sometimes, which is untested (26 human-vs-human games). Comebacks were measured healthy (trailing player wins 25 to 46%, 1,661 replayed games, 2026-11-11), so they are not the reason. Ride along in card rounds where a random effect fits the animal; make it a goal only if human-vs-human play shows newcomers bouncing off good players.
