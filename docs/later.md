# Later

Work parked on purpose. Each item says why it exists and what would make it the next thing, so it is picked up for the right reason and not before.

- **The Open Ocean set's repeated look (Martin, 2026-10-17).** About half the Ocean cards share the same underwater picture: light shafts coming down from the middle of the surface. He wants something done about it eventually; new Ocean pictures avoid it from now, and the existing ones are a consistency pass for when the set is otherwise settled.
- **Few tactical moments (Martin and agent, 2026-11-11).** A puzzle session built from real games found few good puzzles: a strong player sees the best move at once, a weaker one misses it, and even shown it, the reason isn't plain. Reading of why (agent's, not settled): most turns force nothing, so the best move is a judgment about future turns and the hidden hand, not a calculation; chess-style "found it" moments need forcing threats and double threats, and here only the den and a food total near the line force a reply. The lever: more cards that keep acting on their neighbouring crossroads while they stand (traps, auras, zones that put a price on landing nearby, never forbid it: a frontier five spots wide freezes otherwise), and more threats that force a reply. Test: puzzles whose answer is beyond argument become easy to find in real games. Pick it up at the next card round that has room for new effects.
- **Randomness seen before the decision (Martin and agent, 2026-11-11).** Martin: random effects may have been thrown out more than needed. Garfield's distinction: randomness before a decision (choose one of three, a token or food that appears visibly a turn ahead) creates decisions and keeps positions fresh; randomness after a decision (did it work?) replaces them. A hypothesis, not a finding: the case is freshness, and weaker players winning sometimes, which is untested (26 human-vs-human games). Comebacks were measured healthy (trailing player wins 25 to 46%, 1,661 replayed games, 2026-11-11), so they are not the reason. Ride along in card rounds where a random effect fits the animal; make it a goal only if human-vs-human play shows newcomers bouncing off good players. Ben Brode's Marvel Snap GDC talk (2023) adds the case for randomness after a decision when the player chooses the risk (Snap's Danger Room): it gives the player who is behind an "is it worth the risk?" decision, and gives a loser something other than their own play to blame.
- **Collection and economy (Martin and agent, 2026-11-11).** How players get cards and how the game makes money: Martin's principles (no gacha, buy a card directly, one shared currency, achievements that pay out directly, animated premium cards earned by achievements, a PvE challenge per legendary), the research round on other games, his voice note and the agent's response, all in [`collection-and-economy.md`](collection-and-economy.md). Nothing settled. The agent recommends keeping every card unlocked until the first expansion. Pick it up when the player count makes a collection worth gating, or earlier for the one-legendary animation test, which decides whether the cosmetics plan is real.
- **Card shape and board shape (Martin, 2026-11-13).** Martin, as thoughts for the record: maybe the cards should not have a playing card's shape but be closer to rectangles; maybe the board should be closer to a chessboard or a hex board, tiles touching, without so much empty space and graph edges. The agent's reading (not settled): the board's geometry is Go's, not chess's: units stand on the crossroads and the spaces between them are the regions that produce food, held by occupying every corner, so touching tiles change the rules (where a unit stands, what a region is), not only the look; the empty space can be shrunk visually (bigger crossroads, shorter paths, fields that read as land) without that. A board of tiles holding the cards themselves would also remove the card-to-piece change that the card-play animation exists to hide (Snap and Gwent keep the card), against the board's round portraits, which are recorded in design as decided. Pick it up as a design session of its own, not inside an animation or layout pass.
- **Token rims in war paint (agent's proposal, 2026-11-13).** The blue and red enamel rims are the one made-looking object on a board that is otherwise untouched ground. Proposal: keep the rim's geometry exactly (thick ring, boss with the strength, hue, dark outline) and change only its material to the dens' war paint, a brushed band of team-coloured paint with a dab of it as the boss, so team colour is paint everywhere on the board. The risk is contrast (paint edges are soft; thin rims already failed), so it needs a clean inner edge and the outline, measured against today's rim at real 2x before Martin sees it. Martin, 2026-11-13: not now, he doubts it will work and does not want to spend image quota on it. Pick it up only if the rims start to bother him.
