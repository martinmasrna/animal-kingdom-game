## Where this is going

*Animal Kingdom* is a two-player card game Martin is designing, meant to become a digital game people play competitively. The path: design a game that is fun and deep (only humans can judge it), get other people playing it, stabilise the rules and cards, balance it, go public.

**Now: designing the mana game.** On 2026-11-20 Martin made the mana ruleset the whole game ("Literally nothing else than what's currently on the staging server (and localhost 8000) matters") and the old rules, their 71 animals, bots and tools were deleted. The game is set in a world of our own, not on Earth (Martin, 2026-11-19: "I thought about it and it's 1000% the right decision"), and its cards are not traditional Earth animals (Martin, 2026-11-20: "alltogether, at least the traditional Earth animals"). What the cards and the world are instead is open. Every number and rule below is a working version from his playtests, not settled.

What the game is for, why mana, and Martin's words on the rules' shape and the board: [`design.md`](design.md).

## The rules as they play

- **Board:** two maps in `data/maps.json`, picked with `AK_MAP`, your side at the bottom on both; which one the game keeps is open (Martin's reasons: `design.md`). Staging plays `map_sq16`: 16 square tiles, 4 by 4, neighbours across a side only, a region every 2 by 2 block (9). `./play` without `AK_MAP` plays `map_hex19`: 19 pointy-top hex tiles in rows of 3, 4, 5, 4, 3, a region every three mutually adjacent tiles (24). Each player's nearest row is their base, where their connection starts and where either player may place. Behind each base is its den.
- **Mana:** on your Nth turn you have min(N, 10) mana, lost at the end of the turn. A card costs its `cost`; a leader's power costs 3 and can be used once a turn (Martin, 2026-11-21). Anything an effect puts into play is free.
- **Cards:** opening hands of 3 (first player) and 4 (second), a mulligan, one free card at the start of each turn, the first player's first turn included (Martin, 2026-11-21), no other draw.
- **Covering:** a card placed on an enemy it is stronger than covers it, and a covered card, yours or theirs, is removed.
- **Zero strength:** an animal at 0 strength is removed, by the rules (Armor doesn't hold), each time the effects finish resolving (Martin, 2026-11-21: "make it a general rule").
- **Food:** both start at 15; each region (three tiles) a player holds pays 1 food at the end of their turn; 30 food wins, 0 loses. A card placed or moved into the enemy den, connected through one of their base tiles, destroys food equal to its strength (at most what the den holds) and is spent.
- **Fatigue:** a card a player would draw from their own empty deck is not drawn and costs them food instead, 1 the first time, then 2, then 3, and so on; at 0 food they lose at once, on their own turn too. Every draw counts: the turn's free card, a card's draw, the Sage's power (usable on an empty deck for that reason), and the rest of a draw the deck runs out under. Scout on an empty deck and cards an effect puts into a hand are not draws. The loss is neither income nor a raid, so no leader reacts to it. A working rule (Martin, 2026-11-22: "we just add fatigue exactly like in hearthstone, losing energy -1, -2, -3, ...", his answer to games that stalled with both decks empty; "energy" read as food).
- **Turns:** a turn ends when its player ends it; no turn ends or is skipped by itself.
- **Decks:** 25 cards, 2 copies of a card, 1 of a legendary, one leader.

## What exists

- **Engine** (`engine_rs/`, Rust): the mana rules only, deterministic and serializable, validating every action, the one implementation of them. It is the Python module `ak_engine`, built by `./play`, `./test` and `./cards` whenever its source moved; the server, the tools and the tests reach it through `animal_kingdom/game.py`, which holds no rules. About 4 million clone-and-apply pairs a second on one core. How a card's effect is added: `engine_rs/README.md`.
- **Bot** (`engine_rs/src/bots/`, Rust only): the opponent in Practice sends back every opening card that costs more than 2, plans its whole turn, looks first for a win this turn, judges a finished turn as a race to the winning food (weights fitted to who won, refitted with one command after a card change), and scores its twelve best turns against the opponent's reply in three hands they may hold (`web/match.py` `PRACTICE_BOT`); it plays a mirror of your deck, reads only what its seat can see, and reads no card by name. About 18 ms a turn on one core on the square board, 13 on the hex board. The greedy bot the game shipped with (`animal_kingdom/bot.py`) plays any move it fails on. How one bot is measured against another, and where they stand: `tools/bot_bench/README.md`.
- **Ranked** (back on 2026-11-23 at Martin's asking, as it worked before 2026-11-20 with only him and bots; the request reached the building session as a written brief, not in his own words): home's Ranked opens the ranked screen, and its Play searches for a rated match with the deck shown there. Ranked is played between people (Martin, 2026-11-23: "let's just do it as if we had 1000s of real players already"; 2026-11-24: "didn't I tell you to design it for PvP from the get go?"): you meet a person searching from your own league, in Legend one near your rating, and two people play on the turn clock, so one who leaves loses and the match is rated. A bot steps in when no person comes within 15 seconds (`server.py` `RANKED_WAIT`), and at once when nobody else is online, since then there is no one to wait for. While the search takes, Play gives way to a turning ring with the time searched and a cross that cancels; leaving the ranked screen cancels too. The leagues are as they were (`web/tiers.py`: Wood to Diamond and Legend above, three divisions of five wins counting 3, 2, 1, two wins from the third in a row, a loss loses one; below Legend you see your league, in Legend your rating), and so is the rating under them (`web/ladder.py`, Glicko-2, everyone starts at 1500). You meet a bot from your league's band of ratings, or the nearest when the band holds none, never the deck you just met while the pool has another. The bots are one bot at five levels (`web/match.py` `BOT_LEVELS`): level 5 is Practice's, 4 and 3 are the same bot with less search (20,000 positions a turn and no look at the reply; one action at a time), 2 is that one-action search judging with the shipped greedy bot's evaluation and 1 the greedy bot itself. Each level with each deck is a player of its own on the ladder. To the player facing it a bot is called by the band its rating sits in ("Expert Bot") and stands in the league that rating falls in; never by its deck (Martin, 2026-11-23: "obviously you shouldn't see your opponent's deck names"). The leaderboard (a corner button on the ranked screen, `#/leaderboard`; Martin on its mockup, 2026-11-24: "yeah looks good") is one screen for where you are among the leagues and who is in them: every league in a row along the top, yours chosen as it opens and marked with a dot, and under it the chosen league's players, best first, each their place, their name, the wins in their division as five small dots and the division, in Legend their rating; your own row outlined. People are on it from their first rated game and you from the start; a bot stands in the league its rating falls in. The same layout on a wide screen ("I think it looks nicer than 2 columns"), and no leader's picture in a row ("what are the hero icons for? What is the point?"). Their decks are the premade ones (`data/decks.json`, which the bot benchmark also plays) that are legal with today's cards: on 2026-11-23 two of Martin's five, Control and Combo Token (Aggro, Midrange and Food Token hold cards that left on 2026-11-22). Their ratings are measured by `tools/ladder/seed.py` (every bot against every other on staging's map, 1,800 games on 2026-11-23): the levels at 1273, 1374, 1505, 1617 and 1731, the ten bots from 1234 to 1733, so the leagues under Stone and from Ruby up hold no bot of their own. A bot whose deck's cards changed since it was measured stands on its level's rating, less sure, until the seed is measured again (`web/pool.py`); nothing waits on that. The screens (`static/ranked.js`, `ranked.css`) are a grey prototype of the structure Martin went through in mockups on 2026-11-23 and 24 ("greybox-wise it all looks good, we don't have to worry about finetuning the looks and animations right now"); what a league looks like waits for the world's art ("we don't yet know what the leagues will look like"), so a league's mark is a disc in its placeholder colour holding its division's number, and in Legend the rating ("it should be inside it, of course"; the rating and never a place beside it: "we either show Rating or Rank, but not both"). The ranked screen is where you are between matches: your league's mark and name, the five wins of your division, the deck you bring and Play; the standing above and the deck at the thumb on a phone, side by side on a wide screen. Play shows the versus moment over the match as it opens, about 2.5 seconds with nothing to press: each player is one card, the game's own card of their deck's leader with their name on it and their league's mark in its corner. A ranked match ends on two full screens of its own in place of the match screen's end box: the verdict (your leader's picture and the word; Martin, 2026-11-24: "I shouldn't see my rank on the card, that comes after. I also don't have to see my name there"), then your rank, where what the match did lands on your standing as it was going in (a win or a streak's two, a loss, a division, a league that takes the whole screen, the rating counting in Legend), and Continue leads to the ranked screen ("it should land as close to victory screen as possible, and it should be on fullscreen. It should be the highlight, the culmination of the match itself, but the ranked screen is already OUTSIDE of the match"). No word stands where a sign or a state can say it ("the label is laziest, ugliest possible way to do it"): on a streak the two wins the next win brings wait faintly in the row. The old placeholder emblems (`static/kit2/league/`) are drawn nowhere.
- **Web client** (`./play`): home is Ranked (see Ranked), Play (against a friend by code or link), Practice (your deck against the bot) and Collection (build decks; the open deck is a list in the side column). The match screen tilts the board back 40 degrees with the pieces standing out of their tokens, a den behind each base row showing its food, the leader beside its den, the hands at top and bottom. Every piece is the token Martin set in the rim tool (team frame, stand-in figure, his numerals). On the square board the tiles are painted octagons, a square with 12% cut off each corner (`static/sq/`, exported by `design/screen/kit/sqtile/export.py`): a creature stands on its tile, the tile takes its team's colour, and a region's stone lies in the hole where four tiles meet. Its card, on every screen the client draws while the server plays a square map (the hand, reads, the collection, the patch notes, the card table), is the rectangle Martin tuned in `design/screen/square/card/tune/` (2026-11-23: "let's go with what we have now"), its dials one block in `static/mana.js` (`CARD_LOOK`); grey-box surfaces, the leaders on the rail still the hex token's. Each side's food is a ball of light in its team's colour, and food in flight is its team's colour too. While a side has a way into the enemy den (a piece on an enemy base tile connected back to its own base), lines in its colour run from its ball to its base row and from the enemy's base row into the enemy's ball; at rest there are none. On a phone held upright (any window taller than wide) the square board's match has its own screen (`app.js` fitStage, `mana.css` .game.port, a 720 by 1280 stage): the opponent's hand along the top edge beside the controls; the opponent's row (leader and mana left, deck right) level with their food ball; the board tilted back 24 degrees, not 40; your row (leader and mana left, deck and End turn right) level with your ball; your hand along the bottom edge, one row up to six cards, from eight two rows laid like bricks, each front card under the gap between two back ones; the last plays behind a button in the controls' row; the opening hand and a choice's cards two to a row at a size their rules read at. A finger drags a card from the hand onto a tile, or taps it and then the tile; a long press reads a card or a piece. The hex board keeps the desktop screen, scaled, on a phone. A chosen target is shown by Hearthstone's target arrow in the form Martin picked from `design/screen/square/arrow/` (2026-11-23: "blocks reads the best"; "blue/red looks good"), drawn by `static/aim.js`: asked for the enemy a Roar acts on, a blue arrow runs from the unit that roared (or your leader, for a power that targets: the Raider's drain) to the pointer and settles on a ringed target, no prompt but Skip where the choice may be declined; on touch a tap on a target shows it for 0.3 s before the choice goes, and a drag from anywhere on the board aims it; the opponent's choice plays as its own 0.55 s step, a red arrow from their unit to the target before the effect (the engine's `aim` event). Not for playing a card. The whole look is a plain prototype.
- **Patch notes** (`static/patch.js`, a window over the game): numbered patches from 0.0.1 (Martin, 2026-11-21), each the rules that changed as bullets Martin redlined (`web/patches/VERSION.md`) and every card and hero power changed, added or removed since the last patch as the cards before and after (`tools/patch/build.py`, which diffs the game against the last patch's snapshot). A Patch Notes button in home's top bar opens it, and a new patch opens once by itself, on whatever screen the game loads, for a returning player.
- **Card table** (`./cards`): every card's values and how settled each is.
- **Profiles, friends, chat, match history** on the server. Sign-in with Google or Discord, the feedback e-mail and Discord posts are still in the server code but unreachable from the client.
