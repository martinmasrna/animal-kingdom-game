# Status

The map. Read it first each session and keep it current: when something here changes, rewrite the line, don't append to it.

## Where this is going

*Animal Kingdom* is a two-player tactical deckbuilding game Martin is designing, meant to become a digital game people play competitively. The path there:

1. **Design a game that is fun and deep.** Only humans can judge this.
2. **Make it playable by other people**, so humans other than Martin can play and give signal.
3. **Stabilize the rules and card pool.**
4. **Balance it seriously** with the simulation platform.
5. **Go public**, where player data takes over as the balance source.

**Current stage: 1, blocked on one design problem; 2 has a local web client, not yet hosted.**

## What exists

- **Engine** (`animal_kingdom/engine/`): complete rules for the seven-deck pool, deterministic and serializable, validating every action. Solid and well tested.
- **Bots:** random, greedy, turn and referee, plus a learned evaluator. Good enough to be an opponent and to give directional balance reads. They can't plan across turns. See [`bots.md`](bots.md).
- **Simulation:** round-robin reports, paired A/B benchmarks, a deckbuilding optimizer, metagame search, and the baseline-deck ruler. See [`balance.md`](balance.md).
- **Web client** (`./play`, `animal_kingdom/web/`): the menu flow and the horizontal game screen from the design mockups, over the real engine. One game a match (the best-of-3 returns with more maps, `docs/pre-launch.md`) against Easy/Normal/Expert bots (greedy/turn/referee), or against a friend by link or code (two browser tabs work). Rematch lets the loser go first. Every game with a human seat is saved to `results/human_games/web/` in the replay format. Hosted at https://animal-kingdom.fly.dev/ (Fly.io, Frankfurt, about $4 a month) as well as `./play` locally.
- **Terminal interfaces:** a Rich CLI (`./run`) and a Textual recorder (`./record`).

## Open problems, most important first

1. **The goodstuff problem (design).** A pile of the best cards from every deck beats all seven themed decks and nothing beats it, so there is one correct deck instead of an archetype metagame. The direction is synergy payoffs a pile structurally can't reach (family-count thresholds); the Colony prototype was promising but short. This is the game's central open question: [`design/goodstuff.md`](design/goodstuff.md).
2. **Getting other people playing.** The client is live at https://animal-kingdom.fly.dev/ (deployed from the committed HEAD with `deploy/deploy.sh`; profiles, matches and human game logs live on the server's disk, and `deploy/deploy.sh pull` copies the human game logs into `results/human_games/web/` to commit). Players sign in with Google or Discord (keys in Fly secrets; locally in the untracked `results/oauth.json`). The live site runs the build deployed on 2026-09-29 evening; everything since (Roar, Armor, Cairn, the spines, Gale, den) is committed but not deployed. Nobody but Martin has played it yet. The collection is live (`#/collection`, `static/collection.js` + `collection.css`), settled with Martin on 2026-09-29 (design sandbox `builder/notes.md`, round B2): a grid of compact cards (the starters' cards plus the bench, cleared cards in no starter; [`cards/shelved-cards.md`](cards/shelved-cards.md)) under family / strength / rarity / search filters; on the right your decks (a profile starts with the seven starters as its own, at most 20; an unedited copy follows its starter as the starter's list changes, and editing its cards freezes it as the player's own for good), or one deck being edited (Play, n/30, Done). Rename, cover, copy code and delete on the open deck's tile; deck codes (`static/deckcode.js`, append-only card numbers in `static/cardnums.js`) copy and paste, a plain "3x Lion" list too; right-click a card for it large with its keywords explained. Its look (night granite, stone slabs, Fira Sans Condensed, no labels that restate anything) is the model for the other menus, shared through `static/menu.css` and `menu.js`. A first visit learns by playing: home's piece offers only Learn to play (or "I already know how to play"), and after that How to play, on the corner piece, opens the list of lessons, any one to take again. The tutorial (`web/tutorial.py`, `static/tutorial.js`, built 2026-09-30 with Martin's line-by-line review and two cold reviews, Opus and Astra, in the design sandbox's `screen/tutorial-review/`) is two lessons, each a real game on the real map with a fixed deal against a scripted opponent that plays like a player (it uses its moves, puts down weak animals to cover, never wins), written so a bright ten-year-old could follow it (the clarity bar, not the audience): every thing named before use, each coach line either tells and waits for Next (the opponent waits too) or asks for one click, every legal place ringed, the game's own words only. Lesson 1: the dens, placing, the turn's two moves, connection, regions and food, drawing, covering, a Roar; won on 100 food. Lesson 2 starts from a set-up board (your Lion and Buffalo, the opponent's den guarded by three 7s): the glow, Flight, stacks and removal (the opponent's Eagle covers your Squirrel, the Black Mamba frees it: its Roar may target only that Eagle), Apex Predator (the Polar Bear eats a guard); won on the den. Its last button starts a real match, the player's Cats against an Easy bot playing Aggro, whose mulligan says in plain words what it is for. There is no separate Play screen (settled with Martin 2026-09-30, design sandbox `play/`): home carries the match. The key art fills the screen (two groups of the pool's savanna animals facing off across a watering hole, painted at the viewer's time of day: `static/kit2/keyart_{night,dawn,day,dusk}.webp`, made in the design sandbox's `home-art/`), the title along the top and, on the ground, one granite piece: your deck | the opponent (Bot with level and deck, or Friend with a code field), Play under them. The deck slot opens a chooser with your decks beside the list of the deck under the pointer, and Open in collection (`#/collection/<id>`); the collection's Play lands back home with that deck chosen. The places stand together on one granite piece in the top-right corner (settled 2026-09-30): Collection, How to play once the lessons are known, Profile; Leaderboard and Settings join it when they exist. Home shows no title: the painting says what the game is. `#/join/<code>` is the same screen with the friend's match; the gauntlet (ten games against every starter deck) is a developer's tool at `#/gauntlet`. Every player has a profile (`web/profiles.py`, SQLite in the untracked `results/web.db`): a first visit creates a guest with a name#tag, and a sign-in code on the profile screen brings it to another device (no passwords yet; real login before going public). The profile holds the player's decks, which are playable in every mode and checked against the deck rules when played, and the match history (built with Martin 2026-09-30): on the ground one column: a header row with "Match history" and a deck filter (a dropdown in the collection's style, each deck with its games won–lost, "All decks" with the total), then your matches, one row each (won or lost, both decks as board pieces in the team rims, the date; against a bot its deck's name and level, against a person their name, since a person's deck name is theirs and no view carries it). A match opens its replay (`#/replay/<match>`): the game screen, read-only, stepping through every view you saw, with the opponent's hand face up (the game is over; an eye in the controls hides it) (play/pause, back and forward a move skipping steps that change nothing on screen, a progress bar with a mark per turn to click or drag, the arrows and Space; Leave or Escape back to the profile). Your own choices show as they were asked, read-only (the mulligan, a card's prompt); the last few moves stand beside the turn, and clicking one, or any in the history panel, goes to it. A match is one game (no best-of-3 for now), and every match in a history has its replay: the server renders it once when the match ends, while the rules still match, and keeps it gzipped in `results/replays/` (`web/replay.py`); at startup it gives older matches theirs from the game logs and drops from the history any that can't be replayed (old best-of-3s, cards changed since). The gauntlet stays out of the history. Opponents see each other's name#tag. Everything is owned for now: there's no progression yet. Matches between two people run a turn clock (30 s free per decision plus a 3-minute bank; bot games are untimed). Not in the client yet: the Leaderboard/How to play/Settings entries, and placeholder art is in for all seven decks. The game screen is one painted board plate with painted pieces (`static/kit2/`, made in the design sandbox's `screen/kit/`; layout and value plan in its `screen/plan.md`), drawn by `static/board.js` from the plain logic in `static/turn.js`, with animations for landings, removals, draws, the opponent's revealed card and food income; the profile, the friend lobby and the pre-match screen have the menus' look too. Browser and unit tests: `animal_kingdom/web/test/` (see CLAUDE.md). The UI spec for content and states is the mockup set in `~/Work/fun/animal-kingdom-design/` (`game/horizontal.html` with `game/notes.md`, `menu/` with `menu/notes.md`, `builder/`).
3. **The aggro redesign** is mid-flight: Martin's card analysis is in [`cards/aggro-redesign.md`](cards/aggro-redesign.md), a candidate slate in [`cards/aggro-redesign-candidates.md`](cards/aggro-redesign-candidates.md). Done so far: Gale (5, "Flight. Roar: draw a card for each unit you control next to the opponent's den.", 2026-09-29).
4. **Egg Control is Snake/Bird control tied together by their Eggs (2026-09-30).** The Snake Egg and Bird Egg are back with a split payoff (part on the Roar, part at the hatch), replacing Eagle and Viper; Egg Eater (+2 strength for each removed Egg) joins Python and Rattlesnake as the third growing Snake, all three at 2 copies; Owl, Fathom and the Bird Egg use the new card-text term **Scout** (look at three, draw one, shuffle the rest back; [`rules/keywords.md`](rules/keywords.md)). Eon is the Ouroboros (10, Apex, shuffles itself back one smaller each turn). Martin went 16–4 with it against the Expert bot (8–2 each against Aggro and Canine, 2026-09-30) and calls it very strong; the bots underplay it (about 5% against goodstuff under any pilot for the old list), so a low simulated Egg win rate is a pilot flaw until a stronger bot says otherwise. Details in [`cards/decks/egg-control.md`](cards/decks/egg-control.md).
5. **Card pricing pass, family by family.** Anchors are set by Martin's feel, not by bot pricing: GreedyBot overvalues extra cards by about 5 points, and both bots value strength flatter than a player does. Draw is done (anchors in [`design/principles.md`](design/principles.md)); extra placements, removal and food are done. **Lemming** is left as is, to be reworked if it stays the best common in both aggro and goodstuff. Keywords are done. **Polar Bear** (rare, 8, Apex) stays as the benchmark for synergy payoffs ([`design/goodstuff.md`](design/goodstuff.md)). Colony pass done 2026-09-28: kept as is (the swarm reaches 4+ Colony units in 95% of games; its 4+ cards are support, removal and draw, that keep it alive while queens and workers win on food). Ramp pass done 2026-09-29: the Unnamed Giant (a strictly worse Borealis) is in the reserve; the fourth legendary is **Cairn** (a glyptodont, the Ice Age giant armadillo, 10, Armor, costs 15 food), and **Brutus** became 8 without Armor (Martin's note from play). **Borealis** (giant polar bear) is a plain 10 with Apex Predator: past the top of the field (8), extra strength buys almost nothing, so it needs no drawback.
6. **The balance numbers are stale.** The last RefereeBot matrix (2026-09-29 night, [`balance.md`](balance.md)) predates the Food walls, the spines (Porcupine, Hedgehog), Armor (Elephant a plain 9, Cairn replacing Yuka), Apex cover-then-eat and Gale. Martin chose not to re-run it yet.
7. **The bots are the priority (Martin, 2026-09-29)**: his reverse gauntlet went 35–35 against the goodstuff pile where RefereeBot pilots get 26%, so most balance findings may be pilot artifacts. Found (details in [`bots.md`](bots.md)): the hand eval hoards, drawing and holding Roar cards Martin plays, because `effect_readiness` pays for every live Roar in hand; but the same term carries den-race value Aggro and Canine need, so no weight is right for every deck. Now: a small value network over generic hand-and-board features, trained on self-play outcomes and iterated. The round-2 network (trained with extra den-race games) is the first to improve or tie every deck against goodstuff (Colony +15, Food +13.5, Cats +9, Canine +6.5, Aggro, Egg and Ramp tie), on the 2026-09-29 cards. Running unattended since 2026-09-30 16:46: self-play round 3 on current cards, retrain, every deck against goodstuff (results about 23:00), then the full 7×7 proof (results the morning of 2026-10-01); steps and output paths in [`bots.md`](bots.md) under "The value network". Nothing ships until the 7×7 passes; the overnight matrix re-run waits on it.
8. **The card is settled (2026-09-29).** Full-bleed painting inside a rarity edge (graphite, silver, gold with a sheen); a painted driftwood bar with the name, a driftwood banner with the strength in chalk digits, a driftwood panel with the rules in Fira Sans Condensed (`static/card.js`, painted pieces `static/kit2/card_*.webp`, made in the design sandbox's `card2/`).
