# Status

The map. Read it first each session and keep it current: when something here changes, rewrite the line, don't append to it.

## Where this is going

*Animal Kingdom* is a two-player tactical deckbuilding game Martin is designing, meant to become a digital game people play competitively. The path there:

1. **Design a game that is fun and deep.** Only humans can judge this.
2. **Make it playable by other people**, so humans other than Martin can play and give signal.
3. **Stabilize the rules and card pool.**
4. **Balance it seriously** with the simulation platform.
5. **Go public**, where player data takes over as the balance source.

**Current stage: 1, blocked on one design problem; 2 has a local web client, not yet hosted.**

## What exists

- **Engine** (`animal_kingdom/engine/`): complete rules for the seven-deck pool, deterministic and serializable, validating every action. Solid and well tested.
- **Bots:** random, greedy, turn and referee, plus a learned evaluator. Good enough to be an opponent and to give directional balance reads. They can't plan across turns. See [`bots.md`](bots.md).
- **Simulation:** round-robin reports, paired A/B benchmarks, a deckbuilding optimizer, metagame search, and the baseline-deck ruler. See [`balance.md`](balance.md).
- **Web client** (`./play`, `animal_kingdom/web/`): the menu flow and the horizontal game screen from the design mockups, over the real engine. One game a match (the best-of-3 returns with more maps, `docs/pre-launch.md`) against Easy/Normal/Expert bots (greedy/turn/referee), or against a friend by link or code (two browser tabs work). Rematch lets the loser go first. Every game with a human seat is saved to `results/human_games/web/` in the replay format. Hosted at https://animal-kingdom.fly.dev/ (Fly.io, Frankfurt, about $4 a month) as well as `./play` locally.
- **Terminal interfaces:** a Rich CLI (`./run`) and a Textual recorder (`./record`).

## Open problems, most important first

1. **The goodstuff problem (design).** A pile of the best cards from every deck beats all seven themed decks and nothing beats it, so there is one correct deck instead of an archetype metagame. The direction is synergy payoffs a pile structurally can't reach (family-count thresholds); the Colony prototype was promising but short. This is the game's central open question: [`design/goodstuff.md`](design/goodstuff.md).
2. **Getting other people playing.** The client is live at https://animal-kingdom.fly.dev/ (deployed from the committed HEAD with `deploy/deploy.sh`; profiles, matches and human game logs live on the server's disk, and `deploy/deploy.sh pull` copies the human game logs into `results/human_games/web/` to commit). Players sign in with Google or Discord (keys in Fly secrets; locally in the untracked `results/oauth.json`). The live site runs the build deployed on 2026-09-29 evening; everything since (Roar, Armor, Cairn, the spines, Gale, den) is committed but not deployed. Nobody but Martin has played it yet. The collection is live (`#/collection`, `static/collection.js` + `collection.css`), settled with Martin on 2026-09-29 (design sandbox `builder/notes.md`, round B2): a grid of compact cards (the starters' cards plus the bench, cleared cards in no starter; [`cards/shelved-cards.md`](cards/shelved-cards.md)) under family / strength / rarity / search filters; on the right your decks (a profile starts with the seven starters as its own, at most 20), or one deck being edited (Play, n/30, Done). Rename, cover, copy code and delete on the open deck's tile; deck codes (`static/deckcode.js`, append-only card numbers in `static/cardnums.js`) copy and paste, a plain "3x Lion" list too; right-click a card for it large with its keywords explained. Its look (night granite, stone slabs, Fira Sans Condensed, no labels that restate anything) is the model for the other menus, shared through `static/menu.css` and `menu.js`. A first visit learns by playing: home's piece offers only Learn to play (or "I already know how to play"), and How to play stands under Collection after that. The tutorial (`web/tutorial.py`, `static/tutorial.js`, built 2026-09-30) is a real game on the real map with a fixed deal against a gentle opponent (one placement a turn, never covers, never closes a region, never takes the den): a coach on a granite piece teaches one step at a time, the first two turns forced (place, connect, hold a region's corners, draw), then covering, Roar and the open den as each first comes up; the player wins in about six turns. There is no separate Play screen (settled with Martin 2026-09-30, design sandbox `play/`): home carries the match. Over the key art (a placeholder painted to the menu brief, `static/kit2/home_keyart.webp`, until the real painting), the title along the top and, on the ground, one granite piece: your deck | the opponent (Bot with level and deck, or Friend with a code field), Play under them. The deck slot opens a chooser with your decks beside the list of the deck under the pointer, and Open in collection (`#/collection/<id>`); the collection's Play lands back home with that deck chosen. Collection and the profile stand beside the piece for now; where they live is open until the hub holds more (Leaderboard, How to play, Settings). `#/join/<code>` is the same screen with the friend's match; the gauntlet (ten games against every starter deck) is a developer's tool at `#/gauntlet`. Every player has a profile (`web/profiles.py`, SQLite in the untracked `results/web.db`): a first visit creates a guest with a name#tag, and a sign-in code on the profile screen brings it to another device (no passwords yet; real login before going public). The profile holds the player's decks, which are playable in every mode and checked against the deck rules when played, and a history of finished matches. Opponents see each other's name#tag. Everything is owned for now: there's no progression yet. Matches between two people run a turn clock (30 s free per decision plus a 3-minute bank; bot games are untimed). Not in the client yet: the Leaderboard/How to play/Settings entries, and placeholder art is in for all seven decks. The game screen is one painted board plate with painted pieces (`static/kit2/`, made in the design sandbox's `screen/kit/`; layout and value plan in its `screen/plan.md`), drawn by `static/board.js` from the plain logic in `static/turn.js`, with animations for landings, removals, draws, the opponent's revealed card and food income; the profile, the friend lobby and the pre-match screen have the menus' look too. Browser and unit tests: `animal_kingdom/web/test/` (see CLAUDE.md). The UI spec for content and states is the mockup set in `~/Work/fun/animal-kingdom-design/` (`game/horizontal.html` with `game/notes.md`, `menu/` with `menu/notes.md`, `builder/`).
3. **The aggro redesign** is mid-flight: Martin's card analysis is in [`cards/aggro-redesign.md`](cards/aggro-redesign.md), a candidate slate in [`cards/aggro-redesign-candidates.md`](cards/aggro-redesign-candidates.md). Done so far: Gale (5, "Flight. Roar: draw a card for each unit you control next to the opponent's den.", 2026-09-29).
4. **Egg Control is Snake/Bird control tied together by their Eggs (2026-09-30).** The Snake Egg and Bird Egg are back with a split payoff (part on the Roar, part at the hatch), replacing Eagle and Viper; Egg Eater (0, grows 2 with every Egg removed) joins Python and Rattlesnake as the third growing Snake, all three at 2 copies; Owl, Fathom and the Bird Egg use the new card-text term **Scout** (look at three, draw one, shuffle the rest back; [`rules/keywords.md`](rules/keywords.md)). Eon is the Ouroboros (10, Apex, shuffles itself back one smaller each turn). Untested beyond unit tests; bots underplay the deck (about 5% against goodstuff under any pilot, where Martin went 5 of 10), and they value a pending hatch as one event whatever it pays, so Martin's games are the real check. Details in [`cards/decks/egg-control.md`](cards/decks/egg-control.md).
5. **Card pricing pass, family by family.** Anchors are set by Martin's feel, not by bot pricing: GreedyBot overvalues extra cards by about 5 points, and both bots value strength flatter than a player does. Draw is done (anchors in [`design/principles.md`](design/principles.md)); extra placements, removal and food are done. **Lemming** is left as is, to be reworked if it stays the best common in both aggro and goodstuff. Keywords are done. **Polar Bear** (rare, 8, Apex) stays as the benchmark for synergy payoffs ([`design/goodstuff.md`](design/goodstuff.md)). Colony pass done 2026-09-28: kept as is (the swarm reaches 4+ Colony units in 95% of games; its 4+ cards are support, removal and draw, that keep it alive while queens and workers win on food). Ramp pass done 2026-09-29: the Unnamed Giant (a strictly worse Borealis) is in the reserve; the fourth legendary is **Cairn** (a glyptodont, the Ice Age giant armadillo, 10, Armor, costs 15 food), and **Bulwark** became 8 without Armor (Martin's note from play). **Borealis** (giant polar bear) is a plain 10 with Apex Predator: past the top of the field (8), extra strength buys almost nothing, so it needs no drawback.
6. **The balance numbers are stale.** The last RefereeBot matrix (2026-09-29 night, [`balance.md`](balance.md)) predates the Food walls, the spines (Porcupine, Hedgehog), Armor (Elephant a plain 9, Cairn replacing Yuka), Apex cover-then-eat and Gale. Martin chose not to re-run it yet.
7. **The bots are the priority (Martin, 2026-09-29)**: his reverse gauntlet went 35–35 against the goodstuff pile where RefereeBot pilots get 26%, so most balance findings may be pilot artifacts. Found (details in [`bots.md`](bots.md)): the hand eval hoards, drawing and holding Roar cards Martin plays, because `effect_readiness` pays for every live Roar in hand; but the same term carries den-race value Aggro and Canine need, so no weight is right for every deck. Now: a small value network over generic hand-and-board features, trained on self-play outcomes and iterated. Round 1 against the pile (200 paired games a deck): Colony +20.5, Food +8.5, Canine +6.5, Cats +4.5, Ramp −2.5, Egg −2, Aggro −8.5 (its den race). It doesn't pass the improve-or-tie gate, so nothing ships; next is Aggro's den race, then more rounds. Egg stays near 5% for every pilot, far from Martin's 5 of 10: its gap is planning across turns, which no evaluation has touched yet. The overnight matrix re-run waits on a bot that passes.
8. **The card is settled (2026-09-29).** Full-bleed painting inside a rarity edge (graphite, silver, gold with a sheen); a painted driftwood bar with the name, a driftwood banner with the strength in chalk digits, a driftwood panel with the rules in Fira Sans Condensed (`static/card.js`, painted pieces `static/kit2/card_*.webp`, made in the design sandbox's `card2/`). The hand and the collection show compact cards (painting, strength, name); the full card shows on hover. Card text fits three lines at every size, enforced by `web/test/cardtext.test.mjs`; wording conventions are in `rules/keywords.md`.
