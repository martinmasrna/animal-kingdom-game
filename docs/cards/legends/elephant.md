# [ELEPHANT], the bull who walks a plain that isn't there anymore

> He sets his foot where the path has always been.

## Who he is

He is the oldest thing on the savanna. His mother walked him along the old routes when he was small, to the water, the fruit trees and the shade, and an elephant forgets nothing. He has walked them ever since.

But sixty years is a long time. The river moved and the fig trees came down. The waterhole he's heading for dried up before anyone alive was born, and there's a jackal den in the bank now. He doesn't know that, or doesn't care. He's walking through the plain he remembers, and in that plain, none of these small creatures exist.

So nothing he does to them is an attack. He sets his foot down where the path has always been, and whatever made its home there since is gone. He doesn't look down. He isn't cruel. As far as he can tell, he's alone, and has been for a long time.

He arrives at a dry hollow and stands there for a while. Then he turns and goes on to the next place, which may not be there either.

## The card

**Roam** is the old routes. **Removing the small things** isn't aggression: he doesn't know they're there. **Hungry 6** is a huge body going to feeding grounds that have thinned out.

## Character

An old man walking to a house that was pulled down years ago.

## Stretch

Elephants do keep routes and water sources in memory across decades, and old bulls do walk alone. Living in the past is the legend's invention.
