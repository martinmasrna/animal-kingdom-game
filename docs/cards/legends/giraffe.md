# [GIRAFFE], the one who sees first

> Nothing gets tried where she can see, and she can see everything.

Legendary, Savanna, strength 7. Hoofed. Your opponent's hand is revealed. Deck: Hoofed. Name: open.

Internal: a legend is the card's character for naming and art, told from the animals' point of view. No player ever sees this text.

## Who she is

A giraffe whose head is above the clouds. From up there the plain lies open: every lion in the long grass, every hyena working round the back of a herd, every move before it is made. She does nothing with what she sees. She does not need to. Everyone knows she sees, and that is enough: an ambush needs to be unseen, and near her nothing is.

The herds graze under her on purpose. This is true of giraffes anyway; zebra and wildebeest keep close to one because it sees the lions a mile off. With her it is more than that. The herds under her have stopped watching the grass altogether, which no herd on the plain has ever been able to do.

## How she got this way

She was born tall in a tower that was tall, and kept growing after the others stopped. Nobody made her. The plain simply rose to meet her eyes one season, and after that there was no horizon left, only her and what was beyond it.

The card is this. **Your opponent's hand is revealed** is her height: whatever is being planned against the herds, she has already seen it, and so has everyone who stands with her. Strength 7 is a giraffe's kick, which kills lions.

## What the animals say

Two sides say her name, and they do not mean the same thing by it. The herds say it with relief: the one who sees for us, the one the sun reaches first. The hunters say it as a curse, the way a thief says the name of a dog: the one who ruins every ambush, the eye that is always too far up to get under.

Which of the two gave the name the card carries is open (Martin, 2026-10-21: "we don't have to commit upfront").

Real analogue: a giraffe's place in a mixed herd, the lookout the others graze beside. Fiction: the lookout in the crow's nest; Heimdall, who sees a hundred leagues and hears the grass grow, without the god.
