# [SLOTH], the one who makes the evening last

> Around her, everything that happens at dusk happens again.

Legendary, Jungle, strength 4. Your Dusk effects happen twice. Deck: none yet. Name: open.

Internal: a legend is the card's character for naming and art, told from the animals' point of view. No player ever sees this text.

## Who she is

A three-toed sloth hanging in the canopy, so slow that green algae grows in her fur and moths live in it. She wakes at evening, and the evening around her goes as slowly as she does. The moths come out of her fur at dusk, go back in, and come out again. The frogs start singing, stop, and start again. Everything that happens at the end of the day happens twice in her tree, because she's still in the first time when the second one comes.

She isn't doing anything. She never does anything quickly enough to be doing it.

## How she got this way

She has been in the same tree for longer than the tree's neighbours have stood. She was there before the vines; the vines grew round her.

The card is this. **Your Dusk effects happen twice** is the evening around her repeating: everything you have that works at the end of the day, works again. She is not the Tortoise, who slows the whole plain all day; she only makes the evening longer.

## What the animals say

The jungle says her name as a joke about time: "she'll get there by evening". The moths in her fur don't name anything, but they come out twice.

## One moment

Dusk in the canopy. A grey-green lump on a branch. A cloud of small moths lifts off it, settles back, and lifts again.

## Character

*Groundhog Day*, for one evening at a time: it happens again, and nobody minds.

## Names

- **Moss** (the jungle): the green in her fur, the seen thing. One beat, plain, a plant word.
- **Hush** (the jungle): what the evening does around her. Softer, a feeling rather than a mark.

## Stretch

Medium. The slowness is real. The evening happening twice is the legend's invention.
