# [EAGLE], one of the two shadows

> If you see one, you're looking at the wrong one.

Legendary, Forest, strength 3. Bird. Flight. Apex Predator. When this enters your hand, add [EAGLE'S MATE] to your hand. The mate (3, Flight, Apex Predator) shares strength with this while both are in hand. Deck: Primates. Name: open, for both.

Internal: a legend is the card's character for naming and art, told from the animals' point of view. No player ever sees this text.

## Who they are

A pair of golden eagles who have hunted together for longer than any hare in the valley has been alive. Eagles pair for life, and golden eagles sometimes hunt as a pair: one flies high and in the open, where the hare watches it, and the other comes in low along the hillside, where the hare isn't looking. Which one strikes changes every time.

Where one of them is, the other is. They come to you together or not at all.

## How they got this way

Nothing happened to them. They found each other the way eagles do, in a sky dance over the cliffs, and they have built on the same ledge every spring since. The nest is taller than a man now. The hares say their grandparents knew it.

The card is this. **Add [EAGLE'S MATE] to your hand** is the pair: draw one and you have both. **Share their strength while both are in hand** is a pair that grows together. **Flight** and **Apex Predator** are two eagles who come down out of anywhere onto whatever they choose, and eat it.

## What the animals say

The hares and the marmots don't have a name for one of them, only for both: two words that go together, one for the shadow you see and one for the shadow you don't. A marmot says it as an alarm, and every marmot on the slope goes under.

## One moment

A hare on an open hillside sits up and watches an eagle circling high above. Behind it, low along the heather, the other one is coming.

## Character

Bonnie and Clyde: where one of them shows up, the other is already there.

## Names

The pair needs two names that go together, the way Prince Leo and Princess Lea do.

- **High and Low** (the hares): the shadow you see and the one you don't. Plain English, a pair by construction, and it says how they hunt. Their risk is that they read as directions, not names.
- From the record: Arne was the name on an earlier eagle legendary (2026-10-09, reopened later). It is Old Norse for eagle, and Arne and Arna would be the Leo and Lea move on it. It is not English, and it is here only as the record.

## Stretch

None. Golden eagles do pair for life and do hunt in tandem. The card's shared strength is the pair.

## Open

- Which of the two is the female, and whether the card painting shows both.
