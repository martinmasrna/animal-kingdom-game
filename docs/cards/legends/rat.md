# Fat Bob, the Rat

> Everyone eats because of him.

Legendary, City, strength 4. Roar: gain 4 food for each other Rodent you control. Draw a card. Deck: Food Aggro. Name: Fat Bob (draft, Martin, 2026-10-20).

Internal: a legend is the card's character for naming and art, told from the animals' point of view. No player ever sees this text.

## Who he is

The rat who runs the granary. Not the boss of the City; the boss is somebody else, and Bob has never wanted the chair. What Bob has is the warehouse, the sacks, the back door the dogs don't know about, and the count. Every rodent in the quarter eats because he lets them, and he lets them because a fed crew is a loyal crew and a loyal crew is the only wall a fat rat needs.

He is enormous and he is not soft. He got fat the way a capo gets fat: by sitting on the thing everyone else needs. He watches the others eat before he eats himself. He knows who took what, who is owed, who is late. A young rat who steals from the sacks is not punished; he is fed, and then he belongs to Bob.

## How he got this way

He was one of a litter in the mill when the mill still ran. The miller's cats took the fast ones and the brave ones; Bob was neither, and he noticed that the cats came for whoever came for the grain. So he stopped going to the grain and let the grain come to him: he found the crack in the bin, and he sat beside it, and the others had to pass him to get in. That was the whole idea, and it has never needed a second one.

When the mill closed the cats left and the grain stayed. By then every rat in the quarter knew the way in went past Bob. The ones who came to him ate; the ones who tried the roof met the owls. He made the granary the only safe meal in the City, and himself the one who hands it out.

The card is this. **Four food for each other Rodent you control** is the crew eating at his table, and the more of them there are, the richer the table. **Draw a card** is Bob's count: he always knows what is coming in next.

## What the animals say

The City's small animals say his name the way men say a capo's: with a grin, and quietly. Nobody fears him the way they fear the cats or the Roach King; nobody crosses him either, because everyone they know is fed from his sacks. The pigeons say he has never been seen outside the granary and may not fit through the door any more. The mice say he keeps a count of every kernel and has never once been wrong. Both are true.

## Dissent log

- He is the capo, never the don. The Macaque is the City's don (Martin, 2026-10-16: a crime boss, cunning, takes his cut); Bob is Clemenza, the fat loyal one who runs his own kingdom under the boss and cooks for the crew (Martin, 2026-10-20). A second don would collapse the two cards into one.
- The fat is the point of the name and of the picture (Martin, 2026-10-20: Fat is the city word, the mob nickname; Big and Old belong to other animals). Art that paints him merely large misses him.
- The current painting (a rat alone on a pile of grain) reads as a pantry, not a racket (Martin, 2026-10-20). Repaint: night, sacks, the crew eating in the background, Bob watching them eat.
