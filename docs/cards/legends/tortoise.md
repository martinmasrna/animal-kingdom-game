# Mogologolo, the Tortoise

> Nothing hurries while he is here.

Legendary, Savanna, strength 3. Armor. Each player gains at most 20 food per turn. Dusk: gain 5 food. Deck: Giants. Name: Mogologolo (draft, Martin, 2026-10-21): Setswana, "ancient one", from *bogologolo*, the time of long ago. The construction: the oldest name in the world is the one left untranslated (El-ahrairah, Fangorn), a word from the plain's own tongues whose meaning the young no longer carry, long because old names are said slowly.

Internal: a legend is the card's character for naming and art, told from the animals' point of view. No player ever sees this text.

## Who he is

A giant tortoise, older than any tribe's memory of itself. The rim of his shell is chipped where things tried him and gave up; one eye went milky so long ago that no animal alive has seen it clear. He is the one animal on the savanna that nothing hunts: not because nothing can, but because nothing remembers trying.

He moves at the one speed he has, and while he is in a place, the place slows to it. The herds graze shorter, the prides sleep longer, and nobody gets to hurry, his own side least of all. Every evening he eats a little, the way he has every evening since before there were herds to notice.

## How he got this way

He didn't. That is the point of him. Every other legend on the savanna was made by something: a fire, a drought, a fight. He was made by nothing but time, and time is the one thing no one else has enough of.

The chips in the shell are the history of the savanna written on one animal: the teeth of a pride nobody remembers, a hyena six generations dead, the flood that moved the river. Each of them tried him once. None of them tried twice.

The card is this. **Armor** is the shell. The food cap is his pace, and the savanna's pace while he is on it. **Dusk: gain 5 food** is the little he eats every evening, which over a life like his comes to more than any of them will ever eat.

## What the animals say

No animal alive gave him his name. It was handed down: the grandmothers heard it from their grandmothers, and it goes back past where any tribe's stories do. It is the oldest word the animals have, older than their words for most things, and it has been said so many times it has worn smooth. The young say it without knowing what it once meant, the way they say the name of a hill.

Nobody is frightened with him. He is pointed at, the way the old point at the river: that was here before us, and it will be here after.

Real analogue (Martin, 2026-10-21: "sounds good"): Jonathan, the Seychelles giant tortoise on St Helena, hatched around 1832, blind, has outlived every keeper who ever looked after him. Fiction: Treebeard, "don't be hasty"; the world adjusts to him, never the other way. Famous tortoises and turtles to stay clear of: Morla, Great A'Tuin, Master Oogway.
