# Ash, the Honey Badger

> The world cast him out and then wouldn't leave him there.

Legendary, Savanna, strength 5. Armor. Stealth. Poison. Spikes. Deck: Untouchables. Name: Ash (draft, Martin, 2026-10-19).

Internal: a legend is the card's character for naming and art. No player ever sees this text.

## Who he is

A completely hairless honey badger, small and low to the ground, with a little extra muscular density. The fire took his fur when he was a baby, and none of it grew back. His thick, charcoal-dark hide carries a grey trace of the old mantle. A lifetime of failed attempts to kill him has permanently marked his whole body and face: old burns, bite damage and overlapping injuries have left irregular, damaged hide and a worn, battered face. The wounds have closed; the damage remains. He is still strong and alive, enduring the life that left him this way. He lives alone, far past where the herds go. He wants nothing from anyone. He has never started a fight. He has never lost one.

## How he got this way

A dry-season storm put lightning in the grass, and the fire ran over the bank where his den was before his mother got back. The den was shallow; the heat came down through the roof and burned him across his body. He lived through it in the dark with the smoke sitting on him.

That was the first time.

The burn wept for weeks and smelled like meat left out, and every hyena and jackal downwind came sniffing at the den. A mother whose cub draws scavengers to her door has one cub and a lot of enemies, so she took herself out of that arithmetic. Not cruel. An animal's reason.

The burns healed into thick, heavy hide, and his fur never came back. That was **Armor**: nothing gets a grip on him. The scars across it came later, collected when predators tried to kill him and failed.

Everything he met after that tried to finish what the fire started, and each attempt left something in him:

- **Poison.** A mamba bit him while he was still a cub. He slept it off, which no cub should. The venom never left him; whatever bites him now gets it back.
- **Spikes.** He went straight at a porcupine, the way his kind does, and it ran backwards into him. Some quills worked out over the months, some worked deeper, and some are still in him. Nothing that climbs on him comes off clean.
- **Stealth.** After enough of that, he went out past the edge where nobody goes. Not hiding. Just gone from where they look.

He was never angry about any of it. There was no one to be angry at; it was just what the world did.

## What he wants

To be left alone. Not as a preference, not as a philosophy: alone is the only place in his life that never hurt him, and he went out past the edge to have it.

The thing that keeps taking it from him is what surviving made him. A creature nothing can kill is a legend, and a legend draws challengers: every young male who wants a name, every pack that wants a story, everything that hears "unkillable" and thinks "not by me". They come to him. He has never gone looking for one, and he has never let one leave. Then he goes back to being alone, which is all he wanted.

He doesn't want a name. The animals gave him one anyway.

## What the animals say

Nobody knows how old he is; the old lions say their fathers warned them. Kings and queens get titles from their own kind; he has no kind. His name came from the ones who tried him and failed, hyenas, jackals, young lions: the hunters' word for him, passed from the ones who limped back to the ones who hadn't gone yet.

## Art brief

Martin, 2026-10-19: "make him fur-less"; "fur got burned off when he was a baby, then scars got collected later on in life, when the predators tried (and failed) to kill him." Ash stays small: "One of the points of Honey Badgers is that they're NOT massive at all." A little extra muscle is fine. The whole body is hairless, with continuous thick hide and distinct old predator scars. His colours are "very dark grey, almost black" on the body, and a subtly visible former stripe in "normal/lighter grey, but still unmistakebly grey not white." Martin preferred round-three #1's expression and #2's sandstorm, and said "looking ahead rather than turning head back works better." The burns becoming protective hide is the legend's exaggeration. Paint the forward, three-quarter composition from words with only the series style sheet attached. His species and small stature stay readable while his hide, scars and indifference carry the legend. No anatomy photograph: Martin said it "dragged the model towards weird angle." Leave other-species analogies out of the painting prompt.

Visual intent (Martin, 2026-10-19): "Something has been trying to kill him since the day he was born. Nothing succeeded, but it needs to be shown on both of his body and his face." He is "living through it"; a pleasant, slightly smiling animal with even, pretty skin and two scars misses the character. His whole face and body carry accumulated permanent damage, while he remains strong. The agreed dark-grey colours and clearly grey former stripe stay. The setting shows his established present, not an illustration of his childhood fire or individual attacks. Martin wants extraordinary nature around him, a clear large hero and foreground/background separation. The exact setting is open. Dust around his feet is fine. Three rolls of essentially the same picture are not wanted.

## Dissent log

- The first draft made him albino, cast out for being born white. Martin dropped it (2026-10-19): the most stereotyped outcast mark there is, borrowed from *Snow in the Desert*, and it left only white-thing names. The mark has to be made, not born, and visible on the card at a glance.
- Martin's first framing of the want was "one quiet meal"; dropped because it makes him funny, and funny is not the vibe. The meal stays as a detail, not as the motive.
