# [BABOON], the old aunt of the sleeping cliff

> Nobody in her troop sleeps past sunrise twice.

Legendary, Savanna, strength 3. Primate. Dawn: give 2 random animals in your hand +1 strength. Deck: Primates. Name: open.

Internal: a legend is the card's character for naming and art, told from the animals' point of view. No player ever sees this text.

## Who she is

An old female baboon, past having young of her own, grey at the muzzle and short a few teeth. She is not the troop's strongest and has never wanted to lead it. What she has is the morning. Every day at first light, on the cliff where the troop sleeps, she goes along the ledges and cuffs two of the young awake, whichever two are nearest, and runs them up and down the rocks before anyone else has eaten. She doesn't pick favourites. She doesn't pick at all. The two who were nearest that day come down the cliff a little harder than they went to sleep.

## How she got this way

She was one of those young once, under an aunt like her, and she hated it. Then a leopard came up the cliff one night, and the ones who had been run every morning were the ones who got to the high ledge in time. She has been running the young ever since.

The card is this. **Dawn** is the hour she works. **Two random animals in your hand** are the young not yet in the fight, whoever happens to be nearest. **+1 strength** is what one morning on the rocks puts into them; it is small, and it adds up.

## What the animals say

The young of the troop say her name with a groan, the way children say the name of the teacher who makes them run. The grown ones say it differently: every one of them was run by her once. The leopards don't know her name; they only know that this troop gets up the cliff faster than the others.

## One moment

Dawn on the sleeping cliff. The troop is still huddled in grey lumps. One lump moves, walks along the ledge, and slaps two others. They squeal, and then they are running.

## Character

Mickey in *Rocky*: an old trainer who has seen it all, takes whoever is in front of him, and makes him harder than he wants to be. Famous baboon to stay clear of: Rafiki.

## Names

- **Old Cuff** (the troop's young): the slap that wakes you, said with the groan. Fits Martin's shelf shape "Old [XYZ]". Plain English, and it's a fond name, not a fear name.
- **Sunup** (the grown troop): the hour she belongs to. Softer and less of a character. Its risk is that it reads as a word, not a name.

## Stretch

Small. Baboons do sit together in the early sun and groom, and old females carry a troop's knowledge, but no baboon drills the young. The drilling is the legend's invention, built to fit a Dawn buff on cards still in hand. "Random" read as "whoever is nearest" is the honest part: she doesn't choose.
