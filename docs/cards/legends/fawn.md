# [FAWN], the fawn who becomes the stag

> The whole glen still calls him by the name he had in the bracken.

Legendary, Forest, strength 2. Hoofed. Flee. Roar: add a Young Stag to your hand. The Young Stag (4, Flee) adds a Great Stag (8: Roar: return every adjacent enemy weaker than this to its owner's hand). Deck: none yet. Name: open.

Internal: a legend is the card's character for naming and art, told from the animals' point of view. No player ever sees this text.

## Who he is

A spotted fawn lying flat in the bracken where his mother left him, which is what fawns do: they lie still while the mother feeds away, and almost nothing finds them. He runs from everything. Two summers later he is a young stag with his first antlers, still running. Then one autumn he is the great stag of the glen, and in the rut he roars from the ridge and every young buck in the valley walks away.

He never killed anything to get there. He grew up.

## How he got this way

He lived, which is the whole story. Most fawns don't make it through their first year. He did, and so did the yearling, and so did the young stag.

The card is this. **Flee** is the fawn and the young stag running home. **Add a Young Stag to your hand** is a year of growing, and the Young Stag's **Great Stag** is the next. The Great Stag's **return every adjacent enemy weaker than this** is the rut: he doesn't fight the small ones, he sends them away.

## What the animals say

The forest remembers him small. The does who saw the fawn in the bracken still call the great stag by the name he had then, and so does every animal that was in the glen that summer. The young bucks he sends off his ridge have never seen a fawn in him and call him something else.

## One moment

An autumn dawn. A roar comes off the ridge, and in the valley below three young stags with new antlers stop, listen, and turn back into the trees.

## Character

Young Arthur, the Wart, in *The Once and Future King*: the boy nobody looks at twice, who turns out to be the king. Famous deer to stay clear of, very much: Bambi, the fawn who becomes the great prince of the forest. This card is that story, so the name and the art have to keep well away from it.

## Names

- **Dapple** (the does): the spots a fawn has and the great stag has lost. The point is that the glen keeps calling the stag by his baby name. One English word, a seen mark, and it gives the joke a dignity.
- **Bracken** (the does): where he lay hidden, a plant name. It carries the fawn's safety, not his look.

## Stretch

None. A fawn growing into a stag is the card, form by form. The Great Stag's bounce instead of removal matches a stag's rut, where rivals are driven off rather than killed.
