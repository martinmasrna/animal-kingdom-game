# [GORILLA], the silverback on the ridge

> Every young one waiting below comes down a little bigger.

Legendary, Jungle, strength 4. Primate. Roar: give all animals in your hand +1 strength. Deck: Primates. Name: open.

Internal: a legend is the card's character for naming and art, told from the animals' point of view. No player ever sees this text.

## Who he is

A silverback, the grey-backed old male of a gorilla group, and the reason the group is still there. He doesn't do much fighting any more. What he does is arrive: he stands up on the ridge above the clearing where everyone can see him, and every young gorilla below him, every blackback still too small to matter, stands a little taller. They aren't fighting yet. When they do, they fight bigger than they are.

He is not the biggest silverback in the mountains, and the card says so. He doesn't need to be. They believe him.

## How he got this way

His own father was killed when he was young, and the group scattered. He was the one the others came back to, though he was nobody's leader yet. He learned then that the group fights for whoever stands up first, and he has stood up first ever since.

The card is this. **Give all animals in your hand +1 strength** is him on the ridge: the ones still waiting to go in, all of them, made more by seeing him. Strength 4 is a silverback past his prime, and the stretch below.

## What the animals say

His group says his name the way an army says its general's. The other groups in the mountains say it with respect and keep away. The leopards in the forest know a group with him in it is no meal.

## One moment

A clearing in the mist. Four young gorillas are hunched in the nettles. Above them a grey back rises out of the green and stands, and one by one they stand up too.

## Character

Henry V before Agincourt: one speech, and every man waiting becomes more than he was.

## Names

- **Ridge** (his group): where he stands, where they look for him. Plain, one beat, a place in their world.
- **Silver** (the other groups): the seen mark, the grey back. Simple and clear. Its risk is that it names every silverback, not him.

## Stretch

Small for the effect: a silverback steadying the young is real, and a hand buff as courage before a fight is a good fit. Large for the body: a silverback is one of the strongest animals in the jungle, and strength 4 is a middling animal. Read here as age. Rejected for an earlier gorilla card in the record: Drum ("Drum for a gorilla is Acorn for a squirrel"), Monsoon. So no chest-beating name.
