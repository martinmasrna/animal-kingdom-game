# [ZEBRA], the bark that brings them in

> Everyone comes when she calls, and every one brings something.

Legendary, Savanna, strength 4. Hoofed. Roar: draw a card for each of your grazing Hoofed animals (each different card counts once). Deck: Hoofed. Name: open.

Internal: a legend is the card's character for naming and art, told from the animals' point of view. No player ever sees this text.

## Who she is

A zebra mare whose call the whole plain knows. A zebra's alarm is a sharp bark, and every grazer near it lifts its head, whatever its kind. Hers does more: when she barks, the plain comes to her. Wildebeest, gazelle, warthog, buffalo, every different kind that grazes, comes in and stands with her, and each one brings what it saw or smelled that the others didn't.

She isn't the one who sees first; that's the giraffe. She is the one who gathers.

## How she got this way

Her mother was the herd's lead mare, and was killed at a river crossing when the herd scattered and couldn't find each other. She was a foal on the bank, barking. The herd came back to the sound. They've come to it ever since.

The card is this. **Draw a card for each of your grazing Hoofed animals** is the plain coming to her call. **Each different card counts once** is that each kind brings its own news: two of the same animal see the same things.

## What the animals say

Every grazer on the plain knows her call and comes to it, and her name is said with that relief. The herds page proposes this job for her, "the voice that gathers" (`docs/design/societies/herds.md`, a proposal, not settled). The lions call her by the sound they hate most, because after it there's nothing left to stalk.

## One moment

Dusk on the plain, the grazers spread out. A zebra on a low rise barks twice. Heads lift: wildebeest, gazelle, a warthog, a lone buffalo. They all start walking toward her.

## Character

Rick in *Casablanca*: everybody comes to Rick's, and everybody brings something.

## Names

- **Bark** (the lions, said as a curse; the herd, said with relief): her call, which is what both sides hear first. One beat, plain. Its risk is that it reads as a dog's sound.
- From the record: **Skye**, which Martin called one of "the best" of the herds' first list (2026-10-21), never applied. English, two beats with the soft vowel the herd notes warned about. It is here because it is the record, not as my pick.
