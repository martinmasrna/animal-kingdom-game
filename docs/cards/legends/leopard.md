# [LEOPARD], the bogeyman of the herds

> The safe place is where he hunts.

Legendary, Savanna. Apex Predator. Roam. Reach 2. Strength 8 today; Martin's proposed change (2026-10-20): strength 6 and "Whenever this removes an animal, gain 1 strength." Name: open.

Internal: a legend is the card's character for naming and art. No player ever sees this text.

## Who he is

A leopard, and nothing more than a leopard, who has become the thing zebra mothers tell their foals about. He is feared for his method, not his size. Lions are feared in daylight, in the open, for what they are. He is the fear of the dark itself: unseen, arbitrary, and inside the safe place.

He does not take from the edge of the herd, where the old and the lame walk and where every other predator hunts. He takes from the middle, where the foals sleep between their mothers. He is never in the same country twice; a herd that loses one moves on and finds him already there. Nobody has seen him clearly and lived, so every herd describes him differently, and the story is bigger than the animal. He is the only one who knows the animal is just a leopard.

Every kill makes him bigger. He started as an ordinary leopard. He is not one any more, and he is still growing.

## How he got this way

A young leopard with no territory worth the name, pushed into poor country by the prides. Then a drought year: the plains filled with the dying, and for a season the dead lay where they fell. He ate them. He learned that a herd is a herd, and that the middle of it, where everyone sleeps trusting the circle, is no harder to reach than a carcass. When the rains came back and the easy meat ended, he kept the method.

The first foal taken from inside a sleeping herd was a leopard being clever. The hundredth was something the herds had no word for. He never did anything a leopard wouldn't do; he only did it where no leopard had done it before, and kept doing it, and grew.

The card is this biography. **Reach 2** is the middle of the herd. **Roam** is never where he was last night. **Apex Predator** is that nothing stops him once he has chosen. The strength he gains is the legend and the animal growing together: the mothers are right, he is more dangerous than he was last season.

## What he wants

What any leopard wants: his meal and his tree. The horror is entirely the herds' doing. He is not cruel, not angry, not hungry beyond a leopard's hunger, and he would be puzzled by what they have made of him if he ever thought about it, which he does not. The gap between what he is and what they believe he is, is the whole character.

## What the animals say

One animal changed how the whole savanna lives. The night circle, the foals in the centre, the stallions standing watch: all of it is about him, and against him none of it works. The herds stopped believing he is an animal some seasons ago. What the mothers say about him is a rule for the young, not a title: stay in the middle, stay close, and he will not find you. They say it knowing it is not true.

Real analogue (Martin, 2026-10-20: "sounds pretty good"): the man-eating leopard of Rudraprayag, 1918 to 1926. Made by the influenza dead that could not be cremated; killed only at night and only people; came into locked houses; for eight years nobody in five hundred square miles went out after dark; survived poison and a trap until the people decided it was an evil spirit. Shot in 1926: an old male, worn teeth, a few healed wounds. Just a leopard.

## Art

The current painting (a leopard coming through burning grass at dusk) is the one moment he lets himself be seen, which is the last moment. No change needed for the legend. If he is ever repainted: he is bigger than a leopard should be, and the fire is incidental; the dark is his element.

## Dissent log

- An injury as the origin (a hind leg or a lost tail that took his speed, so he learned to hunt the sleeping) was my first pick. Dropped when Martin proposed the growth mechanic: a leopard who grows with every kill starts ordinary and is made by what he does, not by what was done to him. The Rudraprayag leopard was made the same way.
- He has no visible made mark, unlike Ash. His mark is the one the mechanic gives him: size. Open to Martin: whether a card needs a mark the eye reads at a glance, or whether "too big for a leopard, and growing" is enough.
- "What he wants" alternative, dropped: the dark as his possession, hunting to keep it. More mythic; Martin has said poetic is not this card.
