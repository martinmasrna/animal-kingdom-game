# [CUCKOO], the one in the nest who doesn't belong there

> The warbler raised her, and has no other children left.

Legendary, Meadow, strength 6. Bird. Roar: remove an adjacent animal of strength 4 or less. If it was yours, play another animal. Deck: Birds & Snakes. Name: open.

Internal: a legend is the card's character for naming and art, told from the animals' point of view. No player ever sees this text.

## Who she is

A cuckoo. Her mother laid her egg in a reed warbler's nest and flew off. She hatched first, and the first thing she did, blind and naked, was push the warbler's own eggs over the side, one by one, with her back. The warblers fed her anyway. She grew to three times their size and they kept feeding her, standing on her back to reach her mouth.

She is grown now and has never changed. Wherever she lands, something small next to her goes over the side. If it was one of her own, something else takes its place, which is how she came into the world.

## How she got this way

Nobody made her this way. It is how cuckoos are born, and she is the most cuckoo of them: the one whose foster mother is said to have raised her twice, two summers running, and never noticed.

The card is this. **Remove an adjacent animal of strength 4 or less** is the egg over the side of the nest; it has to be something small, and it has to be close. She must do it (`docs/rules/rulings.md`): it is not a choice, it is what she is. **If it was yours, play another animal** is the swap, someone else in the place of the one she pushed out.

## What the animals say

The meadow's small birds have one word for her, and it is a curse. Mothers say it at the nest the way people say a thief's name at a locked door. Her own kind don't name her; she never had kin to do it.

## One moment

A tiny brown warbler stands on the back of a chick three times her size, cramming a caterpillar into its open mouth. The nest has no other chicks in it.

## Character

Tom Ripley: moves into someone else's life, and nobody he replaced is around to object.

## Names

- **Big Sister** (the warbler's word, bitter): what she was supposed to be to the chicks she pushed out. Plain English, and it carries the story without saying it. Its risk is that the irony may read as comedy.
- **Changeling** (the meadow's small birds): the old word for the child left in place of a stolen one. It names her exactly, but it comes from human folklore, and Martin's absolute is words from the animal's world. Mark it as the stretch it is.

## Stretch

None worth marking. The card is the cuckoo's own biology turned into a verb: this is the legend I'm surest of. Cuco was the name on an earlier cuckoo card in the naming record; why it went back to a bracket isn't in what I read.
