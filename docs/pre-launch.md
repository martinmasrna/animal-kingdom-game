# Pre-launch

Decisions and work that must be settled before the game goes public, and not before. Each item says what is open and where it came from.

## The road to an open alpha

Settled with Martin 2026-10-01. Three steps, in order:

1. **Phone pass.** Done 2026-10-01. A phone plays upright only, as the design decided (the design sandbox's `game/notes.md`: horizontal on PC, vertical for phone portrait); held sideways it asks to be turned upright. Upright the board is its own painting on its own layout, never the wide one turned (the design sandbox's `screen/kit/plate/port/notes.md`; `PORT_AT` in `static/board.js`): your den along the bottom, the fruit filling left to right toward its gem, the deck over End turn at the end of the hand's row (a replay's controls take that corner). Touch: a tap picks, a press-and-hold reads a card large, no hover labels, the coach says Tap. The menus stack into one column. Four QA agents (Sonnet) played it on phone sizes; `PHONE=1` on the tutorial's slow test plays both lessons upright by taps. Upright art (2026-10-01, Martin's picks): the key art painted upright (the design sandbox's `home-art/r3/`, candidate B, the night master then dawn, day and dusk from it; `keyart_port_*.webp`) and the deck and End turn's own rock (`screen/kit/ledge/port/`, `ledge_port.webp`).
2. **The ladder, with bots on it (Martin, 2026-10-01), before friends: it works for three players as well as three hundred.** A ranked queue where you don't know your opponent before the match: a person near your rating if one is queued (the queue waits 10 to 20 seconds for one), else a bot near your rating. The bots are ladder players in their own right, each a level with one fixed deck (Expert Cats, Normal Ramp, and so on: 21 of them), on the same leaderboard as people, their ratings moving with their results like anyone's (seeded from bot-vs-bot simulation). Once a match starts the opponent shows as what it is ("Expert Bot · Cats"), never a person's name: human-vs-bot games stay apart from human-vs-human ones in the data. Unranked bot practice stays on home as now. Rating: Glicko-2, updated after every game; new players start at 1500 with a wide uncertainty and show a "?" after the number for their first ten games; the leaderboard ranks by the plain rating and never shows the uncertainty (Martin: nobody cares but agents); draws don't count; no adjustment for who goes first (it evens out over many games). The leaderboard is live from day one, mostly bots and a few people, and is the skeleton of ranked play later. It also answers thin turnout: the queue always has an opponent. Built 2026-10-01 (`web/ladder.py`, `/api/ranked`, `/api/leaderboard`, home's Practice | Ranked | Friend, `#/leaderboard`; grey-box approved by Martin, `ladder/greybox.html` in the design sandbox): the bots sit at level defaults (Easy 1200, Normal 1500, Expert 1800) until seeded with `sim/ladder_seed.py` once round 4 is the web's Expert (`docs/bots.md`). A ranked game ends with Play again (no rematch). Still for the email: home shows the life there is (players online); alpha nights concentrate people in time.
3. **Friends and challenges, for family and close friends** (grey-box approved by Martin 2026-10-01, the design sandbox's `ladder/friends-greybox.html`). A friend link, opened once and confirmed ("Add Martin#4821 as a friend?"), makes two players friends, both ways. Your friends are listed in home's Friend tab (online first, a dot; the others with when they last played) and on the profile, where each can be removed and Add a friend shares your link. Picking a friend makes Play a challenge: an online friend gets a piece on whatever menu they're on (never over a match in play), naming you only, not your deck, with Decline and Play; you wait like the ranked search, and a decline or a minute's silence brings Play back. An offline friend: Play sends a match link through the phone's share sheet (on PC it copies). The code field stays for anyone else. No push notifications, ever (Martin). Not wanted: friends' histories (Martin: pointless); ratings are public on the leaderboard anyway. Built 2026-10-01; settled the next morning: a friend's #tag shows only when another of your friends shares the name; an incoming challenge stands in the middle of the screen (as the concede question); a new player's first ranked swings (e.g. 1500? → 1325?) stay as Glicko gives them. Friend requests from the leaderboard (Martin, 2026-10-01): a person's row has Add friend; the request reaches them as a piece like a challenge, at once or on their next visit, with Not now and Add friend; two players who asked each other are simply friends.

- **The best-of-3 and its maps.** A match is one game for now (Martin, 2026-09-30: the best-of-3 is part of the game's identity, but only once there are multiple maps); `GAMES_TO_WIN` in `web/match.py` brings it back. It needs more maps than Savanna Expanse for the three-map reveal (§14), and a rule for who starts games 2 and 3 (built: the loser of the previous game; the rules only say a coin flip picks the first player, §4.2).
- **The first player's edge.** RefereeBot matrices give the first player 57.7% ([`balance.md`](balance.md)); the best-of-3 with loser-first evened that out, a single game doesn't. Measure it on human games before changing any rule. A rematch lets the loser go first. The second player opens with 4 cards to the first player's 3, and since 2026-10-01 each may mulligan their whole opening hand, 4 and 3 (Hearthstone's rule, Martin; the 57.7% was measured with a mulligan of 3 for both). If the edge still shows in human games, the next simple step is for the second player too.
- **Turn clock values.** Poker-style: a free window per turn (unused time is lost) plus a bank for the whole game. Built with the starting guess, 30 s per decision and a 3-minute bank (`CLOCK_FREE`, `CLOCK_BANK` in `web/match.py`); set the real numbers from games between people.
- **Animal calls.** Each family's call as its animal lands (the recordings, `calls.json` and their CC BY credits in `web/parked_calls/`, out of the served folder; wired in `static/sound.js`) is built but parked (Martin, 2026-10-01): the rest keep playing (landing, covering, removal, fruit, draws, the reveal, picking a card, End turn, your turn, the versus moment, victory and defeat, a match found, a friend's challenge). Moving the files and the credits back into `static/kit2/snd/` and loading `calls.json` in `app.js` boot switches them back on.
- **Keyword explanations.** Right-clicking a card in the collection shows a one-line explanation per keyword (`KEYWORDS` in `web/static/card.js`; the board's keyword badges show the same line on hover): a first draft summarised from [`rules/keywords.md`](rules/keywords.md). Players learn the rules from these lines, so Martin redlines them.
- **A painted cursor.** The game uses the system cursors. A custom one (as Hearthstone's gauntlet) only works as real painted art in the cards' hand; a code-drawn arrow was tried in the collection and removed.
- **Art consistency pass.** The card paintings are placeholders in two finishes: most commons and rares in a painterly gouache hand, most legendaries (and a few commons such as Viper and Python) in a dramatic rendered finish. The rendered finish carries much of the legendary look, and a painterly repaint of five cards (Lobo, Raksha, Python, Eon, Queen Marabunta, 2026-09-30) stripped it: every one read as a common. The pass must decide one coherent hand per rarity without losing what makes each card work (its hook, contrast and, for legendaries, the lift), and never repaint a working card for consistency alone. Audit page: `cards/style-audit.html` in the design sandbox.
- **Look-alike groups beyond the rodents.** On the board a card is a 120 px portrait, so animals that share colour, shape and background can't be told apart at a glance. The rodents were repainted for this (2026-09-30); still unchecked at board size: the Canine deck's tawny dogs (Jackal, Dhole, Dingo, Dog, Wolf, Bush Dog, Hyena, Lobo, Raksha, Clarion), the small cats (Lynx, Caracal, Cougar) and Colony's bees and wasps. Method that worked: a line-up page of every portrait at board and medallion size where Martin clicks away the distinct ones (`cards/rodents.html` in the design sandbox), then repaints that break out by colour, silhouette and background.
- **Hook audit.** Every card's painting should carry one hook a player could name in two words and remember the card by (the Chipmunk is "the strawberry rodent"). Lesser-known animals need it most, since they can't stand on the species alone (the old Gopher). Go through all cards, list the ones without a nameable hook, and repaint those from their card effect and the animal's real behaviour.
- **Darkness audit.** The collection should read bright and happy overall; drama comes from a natural phenomenon and the animal's supercharged trait, not from night by default. Night or dark belongs only where darkness is the card's point (Black Panther's Stealth, Pestis). Flag the paintings that are dark without that reason and decide each; the Lion family's storm and Borealis's aurora are intended.
