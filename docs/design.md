# Design

What the game is for and what Martin has said about its shape, with his words and dates. Decided means he decided it; everything else here is his reaction and stays open. The rules as they play now are in [`STATUS.md`](STATUS.md).

## What a card game is for

Martin's model, restated by an agent and confirmed by him (2026-11-20: "You understand the model way, exactly how I'd put it"):

- **Before the match** I pick how I want to play: the deck is a plan that is mine, known before I sit down, reliable enough to carry out in most games. Winning with it counts double; losing is cushioned because the deck can be fixed. Picking an existing deck and building one are the same act.
- **In the match** I carry the plan out against a hand I can't see, and each turn is a judgement, not a calculation: what is he holding and how much do I respect it, do I spend this card now or keep it for when it's worth more, am I ahead enough to play safe or behind enough to gamble, does the plan still hold here.
- **Across matches** each card is something I learn (how to play it, hold it, play around it), and that carries from deck to deck.
- **What kills it:** luck that can't be prepared for, cards from outside the decks, two players playing solitaire, one heuristic that is right 90% of the time.

The agent's reading, which he agreed with: every in-match decision is about *when*, and reading, bluffing, risk and tempo all rest on a card in hand that is sometimes right to keep. So a rule or a card is judged by whether it makes *when* matter and gives a held card worth. His essay `essay-core.md` (workspace root, 2026-11-14) is the same argument from complexity and depth: as little to learn as possible, as much to master.

Why mana: the old rules' two actions a turn ate two cards a turn, so cards could never be scarce and a held card had no worth. Mana with the two-action limit kept was "worst of both worlds" (2026-11-20).

## The shape of the rules

- **Effects are local.** Every effect acts through the board: "remove an adjacent enemy", never "remove an enemy". No cards that act anywhere from hand (2026-11-20: "If we allows spells from hand, we're losing big part of what makes the indetity of our game -- the hexagonal board, the connectivty and adjacency mechanics"). A role other games give a spell becomes a card on the board acting on what is next to it or connected to it.
- **Strength is four things at once** (2026-11-20): removal (it covers one more size), flexibility (more tiles it may land on), defence (one more size can't cover it) and burn (one more food on a raid). Cards never attack, so two 5s hold two tiles where one 10 holds one. Cost is a liability of its own (dead in hand until affordable, the whole turn spent), so strength per mana rises with cost and decks live mostly at low cost: "there is a reason why in hearthstone vanilla minions end at 7 mana, and why no mana curve is linear".
- **Fast.** "our game is much more fast-paced than hs, and I want to keep it that way" (2026-11-20, on the mana cap; the cap went to 10 later that day).
- **His numbers and readings of 2026-11-20** (open, his starting points): the den raid as the win, starting at 15, 30 wins, 0 loses ("instead of stealing it destroys"); a covered card removed ("covered units don't get shuffled back into the deck, they just get removed"); opening hands "3 for first and 4 for second"; a leader's power at 3 mana; base tiles open to both players (the rule that only the owner could place there was "just dumb"). The card food numbers are his to rescale.
- **Evidence comes from people.** "we will not let bot data drive our decisions" (2026-11-20): a bot plays well enough to show the screen; whether the game is good, his and other people's games decide.
- **Open: raids that steal (Martin's idea, 2026-11-24, a brainstorm; nothing decided).** "what do you think about changing the 'raid' mechanic so that you're *stealing* the energy from your opponent, rather than just destroying it? and then the wincondition is just 'reach 30 energy'". Why 0 needs no rule of its own, his words: "if both players start at 15/30, then so either always have something to steal, or you've won the game, right? Cause total energy only goes up?" That holds while nothing destroys food, so fatigue's food goes to the opponent there. On how to balance raiding against regions, his reactions to four ways: regions paying 2, "anything where regions don't produce exactly 1 by default is not simple"; a winning food above what the two dens start with, "you lose the property of 'always enough total energy to win'"; fewer tiles into the den, "square board is already small enough as it is"; a raid that drains each turn, "no, that wouldn't work". His own idea was the hexagon board again, "where there's much mroe regions an they're easier to control, and where it takes one more placement to reach enemy base and it's also easier to defend the base". To try first: the 4 by 5 square board at 15 and 30 ("okay, I can try 4x5 when I get home"), built as `map_sq20`.
  - Readings (Claude, 2026-11-24, arithmetic, not play). With stealing a raider's own regions and the builder's no longer stand between the raider and the win: the builder still needs 15 plus what was taken, the raider 15 less what its own regions paid, where destroying asks for 15 plus what the builder's paid. Raids never change the two dens' total and regions only raise it, so a game is over by the 29th food produced. Predictions for the 4 by 5, untested: raids land about a turn later, regions pay about a third more (12 against 9), and the middle row's regions are the ones fought over. Dials left if raids still decide too much: the food the dens start with and the food that wins, moved together (20 and 40), which lengthens the game; then the cards that raid.

## The world and the art (open; Martin's thinking, nothing settled)

His words of 2026-11-22, a brainstorm "for the future", not a plan. Agents feed this, never write it.

- **How deep:** "long term doing like actual world building and building your own world is like insanely beneficial"; "if I had to choose between like going deeper on the world building or going like deeper and more detailed on the art, I would choose world building"; "if this is actually feasible, then we have to do it"; and against it, it may be "kind of like saying, you know, I have to build a castle first". "I'm leaning towards going deep on both if it's actually feasible."
- **How a world is built:** "start with like some let's call it like a generator ... some external event or like some physical rules that shape the way this world looks like and works" (his examples: the Souls games, Lovecraft). No stock fantasy: "I want to avoid like the standard fantasy tropes ... elves ... dwarves and goblins ... undead skeletons".
- **Energy and the spectrum:** "I really like this idea of like light and energy"; "everything is like made from like pure energy on like the lowest level, and then it just manifests like in different ways"; the six Wisps as a spectrum, red to purple by cost; "what we're calling the food right now would just be energy ... once you reach like the win condition of like 30 energy ... you like ascend ... if you fall down to 0, then you like run out of energy"; "maybe mana can be energy"; "7 like colored crystals or like diamonds or like gems in colors of rainbow keep like popping up to my mind".
- **Animals turned up:** "what would happen if all animals reach like at least human level intelligence"; "you take evolution for each like species, and then you turn it up to the maximum" (monkeys with six arms; a shark that is "just like one giant head ... giant mouth"). "take inspiration from the animals, but make them into your own world, like what Pokemon did". The two ideas "are not mutually exclusive".
- **Humanish and animalish:** his working theory that people file a character as one or the other; "maybe the mix is the best".
- **The art:** the tilted board stays ("ever since we tried the tilted look, I just think it's the better of the two"); a played card's creature "is going to jump out of the card and is going to be on board". Real 3D models only "if we're doing actual world building". On generated art: "I'm always going to have that feeling that we're producing like AI slop". What works for him: "you creating like a custom tool where you hand me a few knobs to turn is like the best workflow we've had so far"; and for creatures, a tool like Spore's creator: "I don't have to actually paint ... I just click around and slide some ... sliders".

## Factions, colours or classes (open; Martin undecided)

His words of 2026-11-22: "whether like colors, factions, or classes, whatever, it's the same thing gameplay wise. Whether we should have them or not"; "I'm kind of undecided ... I could see both of them working very well if executed well". Decks already get identity from heroes, "which I want to keep in the game", so this "would be more like adding second layer on top". For: "stronger flavor and world building, and just more depth in terms of deck building"; "it actually gives you rules you can use for generating like new cards or new creatures"; it limits what a player must consider, though "it's really just double-edged sword" (he liked five-colour decks most). Against: "it's simpler, easier to balance, less cards to develop, less art to make"; "we need to balance like classes against each other" on top of heroes. On colours with coloured mana: "too obviously copying magic". A form he likes: as Magic's Commander, "each hero has like either one color or two colors". "I still think the gameplay should have precedence."

## The board and the screen

- **Decided:** the board runs bottom to top on every device, your side at the bottom (2026-11-17); blue you, red opponent, one measured hue each (2026-11-18).
- **Decided (2026-11-22): the screen is not held to what a physical board game could do.** On his standard of 2026-11-17 ("Imagine it's a physical board game and we're just trying to translate it into digital one"): "I think this is no longer true. Yeah, for sure let's drop that constraint it's only going to hold us down."
- **Open: the board's grid.** Two boards are in the game: the 4x4 of squares (`map_sq16`, on staging) and the 19 hexagons (`map_hex19`); a third, 4 by 5 squares (`map_sq20`), is the stealing experiment's (above). On the square, Martin, 2026-11-22: "I'm pretty sure we're going to keep it"; on choosing between them, "the rules and gameplay should take priority. 100% agree"; on the look, "the hexagonal one looks nicer and much less 'default', it's just that there were a bunch of problems with it".
  - Why hexagons fail under mana, his words (2026-11-22): "it's super easy to just play a lot of cheap units, cover up all your regions around your base"; "by turn 2 or 3 you would have 6-7 regions around your base all under your control"; "you just need 3 adjacent tiles to cover a single region". And the raid: "it takes long time to reach the enemy base. It's 6 tiles away, and also there's only 3 tiles in front of your opponent's den, so it's not that difficult to defend". A smaller hex board with a wider base (rows of 4-3-4-3-4) he ruled out: "it's still very easy to control the regions".
  - What the board must keep: the two win conditions on one resource. "you can decide which of the win conditions you're going to push for this game, whether you're going to try to increase your food or destroy your opponent's food"; "it's basically 2 win conditions but with the same resource".
  - Why hexagons were chosen (decided 2026-11-16, under the two-action rules: "I played 2 games and I'm convinced, this is 100% the way to go"): on the 3x5 board "the middle column was too powerful", "it controlled every single region, while at the same time being the quickest way to your opponent's den", and adjacency cards were hard to get value from at two actions a turn. Mana removed the two-action limit.
  - A reading (Claude, 2026-11-22), a count on the two maps: a base row and the row in front of it is 7 hex tiles holding 5 regions, or 8 square tiles holding 3; the hex board is 5 rows deep with 3 tiles to enter the den by, the square 4 and 4.
- **Open with the grid: the card's shape.** The hexagonal token as card and piece in one shape was decided for the hex board (2026-11-16; 2026-11-17: "The hexagon IS the card ... Imagine it's a physical board game and we're just trying to translate it into digital one"). For the square board, his reaction of 2026-11-22: "we can just have the cards be like oval portraits, and then what comes out of them is like the creature itself ... it lands on a board"; "the squares are already on the board from the beginning. They just get colored by your team when you land on them".
- **Reactions on the mana screen (2026-11-20):** phone first ("desktop will just have to deal with it somehow, same way marvel snap did"); "food to be center, same way hero you're attacking is in HS. It the one and only windondition. The hero is basically nothing more than decoration"; the leader as scenery; the hand bottom centre (anywhere else is "a design crime"). He rejected a den floating under the board, anything breaking the board's hexagon-of-hexagons silhouette, frames that exist only to fit geometry ("a LOT of dead chrome"), food as a number on the leader's drawer (it reads as strength), and five separate elements on a void. He asked about a tilted board and pointed out that hexagons keep their shape in perspective; the tilt and the standing pieces followed.
- **Reactions on the square board's look (2026-11-22):** of seven shapes for its tiles, "it's definitely octagon"; the corner cut, on a slider, "let's make it 12%"; the food, "let's change the orange glowing balls of light to blue/red based on the team", and "the food that's animated as coming out of regions/cards/hero powers should come in the color of the team". The rounds: `design/screen/square/`.
- **Reactions on the look (2026-11-17), from the animal cards:** the spirit "in spirit of Clash of Clans", "very colorful and cheerful", "childlike without being childish", "playful, not dark and gloomy and aggressive".
