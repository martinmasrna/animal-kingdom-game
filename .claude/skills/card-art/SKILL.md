# Card art

Martin agrees each card's True Name in chat, then only judges finished paintings on a review page. Everything between is yours: brief, ideas, layouts, paintings, checks, page, install, learning. His attention is the scarce resource, then Claude tokens (agents share his subscription; the codex quota is for pictures only, never checks), then the ChatGPT image quota; every step below exists to spend less of them.

A card is made in two steps: its four ideas as composition thumbnails on one sheet (one image), then the one or two layouts that clear the bar painted as finished cards (one image each). The model paints a given composition faithfully and invents one badly, so the composition is decided small, where structure shows at a glance, and the painting keeps it. `D/cards/bar/prompts.md` says why and how to write both; read it before writing anything.

An agent that opens pictures gets at most ~25 of them. Past that, older pictures drop out of its context, it reopens them, and every call rewrites its whole cache: on 2026-10-09 one Sonnet agent given 78 pictures looped for three and a half hours and spent ~89% of a five-hour limit. Look through contact sheets and full-size crops; give picture-heavy work to a subagent with its own budget. A background agent that looks dead is stopped (TaskStop) before it is replaced, and "no agents are running" is said only after checking.

Everything lives in the design repo `~/Work/fun/animal-kingdom-design/` (`D` below), except the game's art files. Read before starting: `D/cards/art-principles.md` (what the art must do; the only principles doc), `D/cards/bar/prompts.md` (how ideas and paintings are written), `D/cards/true-names.md`, and `python3 D/cards/bar/art.py status` (where every card stands).

Tools, all in `D/cards/bar/`: `art.py` (every step; run it with no arguments for its commands), `gen2.sh` (Luna as a verbatim proxy to the image model; `BARE=1` sends a text as the whole prompt), `paint.sh` (paints a finished card from a layout, the medium named, settled cards attached for the hand), `edit.sh` (a surface fix on a finished painting: a stray horn, a sky, a colour; never a pose), `manifest.jsonl` (every attempt, review and pick, tracked), `review.html` (the review page template), `install.sh`. Pictures land in `bar/r1/<id>.png` with the log beside them; cut layouts in `bar/layouts/`.

## A batch, end to end

A batch is one habitat and one rarity (commons first, then rares, then legendaries), named `<habitat>-<rarity>-<n>` (`savanna-rare-3`).

1. **True Names.** Every card in the batch needs a line in `true-names.md`. Missing ones: propose one line per card in chat, following that file's rules, and get Martin's agreement before any picture. Write them in, commit.
2. **The habitat block.** `art-principles.md` needs a "<Habitat> <rarity>" line under "The prompt blocks" (`art.py gen`, `sheets` and `paint` refuse without it). A new one: draft it from the habitat's description, agree it with Martin, then test the look on 2-3 sheets before the batch.
3. **Ideas, by you.** For each card, a brief and four ideas, one sentence each, as `prompts.md` says (scene, light, place, what the hero does, where any other animal is; no camera words, no faces, the hard parts designed out). Write them yourself, fresh, from the True Name and the card's history: no writer agent, because every relay between Martin's reaction and the image model loses part of it. Jobs file `D/cards/bar/jobs/<batch>-sheets.jsonl`; brief lines go through `art.py gen` (a file of only briefs records them).
4. **Sheets.** `python3 D/cards/bar/art.py sheets <jobs file> -p 4` in the background (~1-2 minutes each; `done <id>` or `FAIL <id>`, and a FAIL names the quota reset).
5. **Choose layouts, by you.** Open each sheet at full size, one per card. Choose one or two layouts per card by the bar in `prompts.md` ("Choosing layouts"): the True Name in half a second, one clear hero, separate shapes where bodies touch, nobody performing for the viewer, different light across the batch. A structural flaw on the sheet is copied into the painting, so it is caught here: choose another layout, name the fix in the painting's sentence, or write a new sheet. Taste between good layouts is Martin's: paint both.
6. **Paint.** A jobs file of paintings (`sheet`, `cell` 0-3, `picture`, `refs`: one fur-brushwork card and one card whose light matches the scene, never the card's own art) and `art.py paint <file> -p 6` in the background. To repaint a composition Martin already approved, give `layout` (a card id, attempt id or path) instead of `sheet`/`cell`.
7. **Check, by you.** For every painting, crop at full size with `art.py crop <id> x0 y0 x1 y1` every face (an animal eye is dark or amber with almost no white), every set of legs (count them), every contact point; several crops to one sheet. A surface flaw: `edit.sh` (`SRC=<png> edit.sh <card> <new id> "<the one change>"`). A structural flaw: paint the layout again with the flaw designed out in the sentence, or another layout. A painting you'd still cut is recorded with `art.py gate <file>` (`{"<id>": {"tells": "fail: <one line why>", "anatomy": "pass", "near": true}}`); the page shows it under the card with that line, so Martin can overrule you. Never judge taste here. A Sonnet judge on blind crops can point you at things to look at (it caught two of three known failures and failed a settled card), never cut by itself.
8. **Review page.** `art.py page <batch>-r<round> <ids...>` with every painting of the round builds `results/card-art/<review>/index.html` in the game repo and its `files.json`; under each card it shows the sheet the paintings came from, so Martin can ask for another layout by number. Publish it with the Artifact tool: `file_path` that index.html, `files` from files.json, and the `url` of the card-art review artifact (its link is in `D/cards/notes.md`; publishing to it keeps the page's database). Four to eight cards a page. Send Martin the link and stop; no pick of yours, no captions about flaws.
9. **Verdicts.** When Martin says he has submitted: `ArtifactData` `list` of collection `verdicts` on the page's url, save the result as JSON, `art.py verdicts <review> <file>`. It prints each card's pick and note. A note is a feeling put into words: translate it, don't execute it literally. A layout number in a note means: paint that layout (step 6) for the next page. A card picked "none" goes back to step 3 with its notes, and gets new ideas, not reworded ones.
10. **Install** each pick. First look at the pick's anatomy yourself again: crop its legs and every place two animals touch, count legs, heads, horns; a flaw goes to Martin in one line before installing (he may still want it, or a surface fix by `edit.sh`). Then `D/cards/bar/install.sh <card_id> <candidate_id>`: the master into `D/cards/<card>.png|jpg`, the game's `static/art/<card>.jpg` and `.webp`, the pick into the manifest, and a first guess at `CROP`/`STRIP`/`FULL` in `static/art.js`. Check the guess against the picture (the head in the portrait, the animal in the card window), correct it, then run the web fast suite (`cd animal_kingdom/web/test && node --test`) in the background. Commit both repos by explicit paths: design repo `cards/<card>.png cards/<card>.jpg cards/bar/manifest.jsonl`, game repo `animal_kingdom/web/static/art/<card>.jpg animal_kingdom/web/static/art/<card>.webp animal_kingdom/web/static/art.js` (game-repo art commits stay on the local `new-cards` branch, never pushed or merged).
11. **Done.** A card is done when Martin picks a painting. A pick with one named flaw gets one fix, then it is done; nobody keeps polishing a settled card.
12. **Learn**, after each review round: `art.py stats` refreshes "What wins" in `prompts.md`. Every few rounds, a fresh Sonnet agent with the verdicts (`art.py status <batch>`), the stats, `prompts.md` and `art-principles.md` looks for what in the notes is a rule the ideas or layouts should have followed. Apply what holds up to `prompts.md` yourself (commit it), written as current state, one paragraph per line; a change to the frozen style block or to `art-principles.md` goes to Martin in chat as a proposal first.

## Experiments

`art.py gen` makes a single picture from one prompt (the habitat block, the picture text, the frozen style block) for experiments; a job's `arm` labels an experiment arm and `raw: true` sends a text unchanged. Arms are never shown to Martin; the manifest holds them. Every prompt the image model actually received is in the codex session logs (`~/.codex/sessions/`, the `image_gen` call's `prompt`); when a result can't be explained by what you wrote, read what was sent.
