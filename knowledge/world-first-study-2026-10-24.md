# World first: what Magic, Riot and the animal worlds actually do (study, 2026-10-24)

Read this before writing anything about the game's world: a society, a namer, a legend's "what the animals say", a world page. It is the first step named in `docs/STATUS.md` open problem 9: a study of how world-first design is run elsewhere, so that the method is copied and not the surface. It decides nothing. Every society, namer and page shape in it is a proposal for Martin to attack; nothing here is settled until he says so (workspace `AGENTS.md`, "What a document may call settled").

Evidence: six source files in `world-first/sources/` (Magic's process, Bloomburrow, Riot and Legends of Runeterra, animal fiction, small-team creature games, the craft of the bible), written 2026-10-24 by six research agents from primary pages, each marking what the source says and what it infers. The agents' fetch tool summarised pages, so I re-read the six load-bearing pages verbatim before quoting them here; quotes below are from those re-reads unless marked. Claims carry their standing: *several sources* (independent cases agree), *one source*, *inference* (mine, from the cases), *our record* (this repo's own files).

## The one sentence

Nobody builds the world first. What every world that worked built first was a small generator per society (its position against its neighbours, the one bargain it lives by, the verb it does every turn and the weakness that comes with it, who names whom and from what) and everything else as a card, a book or a champion forced it; and our own record says the legends that came easily had exactly that generator and the ones that fail lack it, not lore.

## 1. The premise, corrected

The Oct 21 note says "Magic builds the plane and its world guide before the cards, Riot writes a region bible before a champion." Neither is true as stated.

**Magic** overlaps worldbuilding with design from the first day; there is no phase in which the world exists and the mechanics wait. Rosewater: "there's always a representative of world building who's on the exploratory team and the vision design team", and exploratory worldbuilding "starts like a month before we start exploratory" and runs through vision design ([Drive to Work 1012](https://podscripts.co/podcasts/magic-the-gathering-drive-to-work-podcast/1012-working-with-worldbuilding), verbatim). The style guide exists for one reason he states plainly: without it two artists asked to draw the same thing would each imagine their own, and "the chance of it looking like it's from the same world is very slight." It is a convergence tool for a hundred illustrators, built mostly of pictures. On the card, the order is concept, then name, then flavour text, then image direction; names are written last, by a named role, from the concept (sources/magic-process.md, section 4; *one source family*, Rosewater's columns). Bloomburrow, the closest published analogue to our game, was "a very bottom-up structure" of ten colour pairs with the animal flavour layered on (sources/bloomburrow.md, section 2).

**Riot** did the reverse of writing a bible first. The 2014 reboot removed a frame (the Institute of War, which made champions "puppets") and left the regions that already lay scattered through champion bios; in 2018 Riot's own post called the lore's handling until then "somewhat haphazard", and the lore review that treats contradictions as bugs started at the end of 2017. No public source shows a region bible, its length or its readers. Ixtal, the region most often cited as "written", was written through three champions in sequence (sources/riot.md, sections 1, 2, 4; *one source family*, Riot's posts via mirrors).

**The small teams and the authors** wrote even less. Root's factions start as one sentence of social position; Hollow Knight had "one large chart" and a cast; Rain World began as three creatures and a feeling; Adams built Lapine "as I went"; Petersen wrote Mouse Guard's world when the role-playing-game designer asked him how money works; Warriors' editor keeps outlines and lists, not a bible, and Series One had no family tree because only six books were planned. The one team that wrote a world first, Flesh and Blood, took seven years (sources/small-team-games.md, section 2; sources/animal-fiction.md, section 3; *several sources*).

So the sentence to carry is not "world before cards". It is: **concept before name, society before individual, and the society written to the size of the next card.** That is what Magic's order, Riot's naming goals and Root's one-line positions have in common (*inference*).

## 2. What the generator contains: the cases compared

Each case below did something different on the surface and the same thing underneath. Compare them; the shared skeleton is the page.

**Root (a faction is a position).** Wehrle defines a new faction against the existing cast: Root's earlier factions have "broad governing authority" or "distributed bottom-up leadership", so the Warlord is a single charismatic leader. Each rule then expresses one clause of that position, and the matching weakness (he overextends; his successor is diminished) is what makes it read as a character instead of a toolkit. Where incentives contradicted the role, the faction stopped reading: the Badgers were meant to be a wall of teeth and were made useful to others, "Why slam into their wall of teeth if you can benefit from it?", and the semi-cooperation was cut. A rule that produced no event other players could see (the Council's "bat poker") failed too (sources/small-team-games.md, section 1; *one source family*, Leder's diaries; quotes via the agent's fetch).

**Bloomburrow (a species is a stereotype turned into a verb).** Each animalfolk is a one-line reputation a stranger already holds, converted into a game verb: squirrels "bury acorns and dig them back up later" became graveyard recursion; rabbits "huge families, reproduce quickly" became go-wide. The published Rabbitfolk entry is 853 words (re-read verbatim): a two-sentence reputation with best and worst case ("At their best, rabbitfolk are stalwart, hospitable, and peaceful. At their worst, they are stubborn, narrowminded, and cowardly"), a values line in their own voice ("It's the way it should be"), who holds authority (the eldest in the Fluffle), one relationship of kinship (mice) and one of friction (lizards), and then four sections we would not need, on gear, magic, structures and jobs. Ten species sit under one shared history (the Season of Chaos, banding together underground) so each page states only how it differs. Rosewater's hindsight a year later: "Factions should have been about something that could group together numerous animals", one species per faction "was too limiting" ([State of Design 2025](https://magic.wizards.com/en/news/making-magic/state-of-design-2025), verbatim). The animals with no society, the Calamity Beasts, are named by what they do to the world (Blizzard Elk) and described only in a witness's voice, one quote each (sources/bloomburrow.md; *one source family*, Wizards' pages).

**Watership Down (a society is one bargain).** Each warren is one trade, learned in one scene: Sandleford's complacency, Cowslip's comfort for silence about the snares, Efrafa's safety for obedience, Hazel's warren where the leader listens. A rabbit's Lapine name is a seen mark (Thlayli, "Fur-head") with a status suffix (Hazel-rah), and the English name is the translation the reader needs (sources/animal-fiction.md, sections 1 and 2; *secondary sources*).

**Warriors (the naming rule is the society document).** Holmes: "It was decided very early on that we would have a hierarchy of changing names to signify each stage of a Clan cat's life. Otherwise, we envisioned having to explain that this cat was a warrior, this one an apprentice, this one a leader every time we encountered a character." Kit names are "things or actions that would believably be familiar to a cat living in the wild", the natural elements "must commonly exist in the United Kingdom", and Tigerclaw was Hammerclaw until "the very last detail we changed before publication, when someone commented that cats knowing what a hammer is was strange" ([Warriors story team](https://warriorcats.com/content/article/editors-and-choosing-warrior-names), verbatim). The check that caught it was a human, late; a written list would have caught it at draft (*inference*, the agent's and mine).

**Riot (goals before names, then volume).** Narrative writers set "three to five champion-specific goals, listed in order of importance" before any name (Camille: "Evokes aristocratic eccentricity", "Should be attractive sounding, but not beautiful sounding"), because "it gives us a better way to measure whether the name is a good fit" when everyone on the team has an opinion. A namestorm is "usually around 100-200" names, cut to 25 to 100, then about ten for localisation, and when none feels right the writers "look at the names people liked the most, try to find what they have in common, and then use that to come up with a new list." Regional rules "if they're written down at all, are intentionally vague because languages are pretty flexible" ([Riot, Naming New Champions](https://www.surrenderat20.net/2017/12/red-post-collection-battlecast-illaoi_26.html?m=1), verbatim via mirror).

**Hades and Hollow Knight (a world is a cast).** Kasavin's Olympians as "one big dysfunctional family"; Gibson: "A world can just mean a cast of interesting characters in a small setting." Character came from the relationship structure, not from geography or history (sources/bible-craft.md, section 5; *one source each*).

Put side by side, the skeleton every case shares is five things, and only five (*inference*):

1. **Position against the neighbours.** Who they are relative to the others on the same ground (Root; Bloomburrow's two relationships per species; Hades' family).
2. **The bargain.** The one trade the society lives by, said in one sentence a member would say (Efrafa; "It's the way it should be").
3. **The verb and its weakness.** What they do every turn and what it costs them (Root; squirrels bury and dig up; Mouse Guard's Nature stat, where doing Guard work against mouse nature costs something).
4. **Who names whom, from what.** The namer, the stock the names come from, and the words the namer cannot know (Warriors; Lapine's seen marks; Riot's goals).
5. **One event.** The thing that happened that the others still react to (the Season of Chaos; the Threarah's dismissed warning; Ash's fire).

Everything else a Bloomburrow entry or a Magic style guide carries (looks, homes, crafts, costume, weapons, architecture) exists because their animals wear clothes and their artists need to draw the same windmill. Ours are real animals; looks and home come free from the species, and the painting rules already live in `design/cards/art-principles.md`.

## 3. Our own record, read through the skeleton

The five legends in `docs/cards/legends/` and the naming record are the only evidence about this game, and they fit the skeleton exactly (*our record*).

**Ash** (name on the card in one sitting, art in one round, Martin 2026-10-19): position, outside every society, "past the edge where nobody goes"; bargain, alone is the only place that never hurt him; verb and weakness, never starts a fight and never loses one, and the legend that makes draws challengers; namer, "the ones who tried him and failed", the hunters' word passed from the ones who limped back; event, the fire. All five, and the keywords became biography (Armor is the hide, Poison the mamba) rather than the biography being invented to fit.

**The Hive court** (Melissia and Solenia in minutes): a court is a frame the player already half-holds, so the namer (a court names its queens by a classical given name) and the position (a queen among a caste) came with the frame. That is Bloomburrow's "animals are people" shortcut used once, where the real animal happens to live as a court.

**Azamandulai**: no society at all, so he was named the way the Calamity Beasts are, by what he does to the others (nothing hurries while he is here) in a word handed down by witnesses who no longer know what it meant. The sources would predict that route for every loner (*inference*).

**The Leopard**: the legend has the namer already, the zebra mothers whose rule for the young is about him, and the position (the fear of the dark, against the lions' fear of daylight), but the name is open because the legend was written on Oct 20 and 21 and no naming pass has read it yet. On the skeleton, this card is ready; the herds are not.

**The herds** (Giraffe, Zebra, Wildebeest; the failed sitting of Oct 21): no bargain, no position against the hunters stated as a society, and no namer: "the herds are a society of three ... to be named together, as birth names" assumed kin-naming, and the attempt went to coined names in one invented sound, which went "to mush" (the giraffe legend's dissent log). That is Lapine without the novel to hold it up, which is the naming skill's own finding. The one thing the giraffe legend does say about naming, that the herds say her name with relief and the hunters as a curse, is the namer question, and Martin left it open ("we don't have to commit upfront", 2026-10-21). A herd is a mixed-species society (Mouse Guard and Farthing Wood are the models: several species in one society, each fixed by a job or a pact), and the sources' lesson there is that a pact nobody pays for is noticed (Farthing Wood's predators who cannot eat). Whatever the herd's bargain is, it has to be one the zebra and the wildebeest visibly pay.

**Fat Bob**: the City street is a mob frame, like the Hive is a court frame, so the namer came at once (the street word is Fat) and the second half failed because it was generic: a mob nickname is a seen thing (Kipling's Lungri, "the Lame One"; Thlayli's tuft), and "Bob" is not one. Half the generator produced half a name.

The naming diagnosis of Oct 16 found that rules written from verdicts reject most of the names Martin loves and that what predicted a hit was breadth, several unrelated sources in one list. A society page is not a rule in that sense. A rule is a filter on candidates; the page is a source of them: it says who is speaking the name and with what stake, which is what Riot's goals do and what every gold name in the record had behind it (the hunters' word, the court's register, the street's nickname). Riot's own volume (100 to 200 names, then regenerate from what the favourites share) is the same breadth the diagnosis found, run after the goals, not instead of them (*inference*).

## 4. What the world must not become

The sources are as clear on failure as on method, and each failure has a version waiting for us.

- **Species as the unit.** Redwall's "you can tell a black hat from a white hat just by species" is the cost of the most legible choice, and Rosewater's "factions should have been about something that could group together numerous animals" is the same lesson from the card side (*several sources*). Our decks already group by family and archetype, not species; the society should too. "The lions are noble" is not a page.
- **The stereotype as the default.** Elliott's dissent against thin worlds is that they default to the category mean; for an animal the mean is its stereotype (lion regal, rat dirty), and Martin's record runs against it every time: the albino Ash was dropped as "the most stereotyped outcast mark there is", the Leopard is the fear of the dark set against the lions' fear of daylight, the Tortoise is the first thing and not the last. A page's job is to move the animal one notch off its stereotype, the way the naming skill's Geralt move takes a real name one notch off its stock (*inference* from *one source* plus *our record*).
- **Overbuilding.** Harrison's "clomping foot of nerdism", Sanderson's "expand what you have before you add something new", Hemingway's warning that omission from ignorance leaves "hollow places" (so the page must contain what the cards will test and nothing they won't). The agents' own note is the one to take seriously: a human worldbuilder stops when tired; an agent does not, and Rosewater ties worldbuilding depth to creative-team hours. The size cap has to be written into the page's form, not left to judgment (*several sources*; *inference*).
- **Lore outside the artefact.** Destiny's Grimoire cards, lore that could not be read in the game, were dropped. If the society is not carried by the name, the painting and the card's line, it is not in the game, and the page was for us only. The fiction sources say what a single scene can carry: a rule said aloud, a mark on a body, a refusal, a fear; never a history (sources/animal-fiction.md, section 4; *inference*).
- **The world dictating effects.** When mechanics and world fought at Wizards, mechanics usually won and the world was bent (Lizards flavoured darker to justify black; Birds given "nonflying matters" against the all-Birds deck), and a good world did not rescue weak mechanics (original Kamigawa, Lorwyn's linearity). Our effects come from play, through the deck-design skill; the world's job on effects is the one Root shows: explain the effect in character ("The card is this", as all five legends do) and check that the incentives agree with the role, the Badgers' test. It does not generate effects (*several sources*).

## 5. The unit of society for this game

Settled in discussion with Martin on 2026-10-24, after two wrong units in one day (habitat plus biology, which gives five hundred pages; the family tag, which puts the hippo in the herd):

1. A society is a way of living together that is also one playstyle: herd, hive, pride, pack, school, street, loner. Never a habitat, a tag or a species. That lands at about one page per family archetype, ten or so, which is the Bloomburrow lesson (factions that group numerous animals) in our terms. The Colony is one society although bees do not live with ants: one way of living under a queen, one playstyle.
2. An animal belongs if a nature fan pictures it living that way and its cards play that way. Deer, boar and okapi are herd animals in the game and nobody pictures them otherwise; the hippo's card neither grazes nor holds ground with others and nobody pictures it in a herd, so it is out.
3. Habitat never splits a society. Where they live is the painting's business (`design/cards/art-principles.md`).
4. A card sits in one society; a precon borrowing a card from elsewhere does not make it a member.
5. A society too small for a page gets a paragraph on its neighbour's page, or nothing.

The animals with no society (Ash, Azamandulai, the Leopard, Eon, Iris) are named from outside, by what they do to the others, as the Calamity Beasts are.

## 6. The page: a proposal (revised the same day)

Martin's correction, 2026-10-24: for real animals the bargain and the event in section 2 are evolution. A herd's safety in numbers, a hive's queen, a pride's house are not bargains anyone struck; they are what the animal does, and a nature fan already knows them. So the page's content is observed, not invented, which does not mean it goes unwritten: it is the one place where the deck's playstyle, the cards' effects, the art and the names read from the same source, and the effects are the most important reader, not an exception.

One page per society, written when a family or a card forces it, about a page:

1. **What they are**, as a nature fan pictures them (one or two sentences).
2. **How they live.** The real behaviours, one line each, each a thing you could watch in a documentary: for the herd, mixed species graze together because each sees or smells differently; the giraffe is the lookout the others stand under; alarm calls cross species; the young are kept in the middle; gazelles stot and scatter, and a scattered herd is a dead one; the route is remembered by the old. Nothing invented, nothing debatable.
3. **The playstyle the family's cards serve, read from that.** A family is not a deck (Martin, 2026-10-24: the precon teaches the mechanics; nobody is made to play all hoofed animals together, a few of them go into a defensive or long-game deck the way fish go into a token deck). So this line names the verb a player gets from running these animals, and its cost, in one sentence (the herd: safe and productive together, broken when scattered; regions that hold it produce more food, Flee saves the animal and loses the ground). It is the line `docs/design/families.md` already carries as "how it wins"; the page says where it comes from. Check: no two families share a verb.
4. **Who does what.** The roles the behaviours imply, which are card slots: the eyes (Giraffe, the opponent's hand revealed), the voice that gathers (Zebra), the memory of the route (Wildebeest). This is the effect generator: when a slot is open, the deck-design skill looks at the behaviour list before anything else, and a candidate effect that no behaviour of the society explains is suspect. The Magic caution stands as a caution only: play decides, the page proposes.
5. **Who names whom.** The namer, their stock (a seen mark, a deed, a title, the oldest word, the hunters' word), one or two existing names as the ear's anchor, and the words that namer cannot know. The one invented line in the page, since animals do not name; it feeds the naming skill's goals directly.
6. **Ledger.** Cards and legends that read from the page.

Dropped from the first draft: the bargain as a field (it is line 3, derived from line 2) and the event (it belongs to the individual, in the legend: the fire, the drought year, the mill closing). Nothing about looks or home: the species supplies them and `design/cards/art-principles.md` rules the painting.

## 7. How the page feeds the four jobs

**Effects and playstyle.** Line 3 is the family's identity in any deck and line 4 the roles its cards fill. A new card for the family starts from a behaviour on line 2 and a role on line 4; the deck-design skill then plays it on the real map, in the decks players would actually put it in, which is where it is judged. A legend reads its card's effect back into the character ("The card is this", as all five legends do).

**Names.** Line 5 becomes three to five ranked goals per name (Riot), written before the pool, into the card's legend file, not onto the society page (Martin's redline, 2026-10-24: a card's goals don't belong on the family's page). The naming skill's wide pool from unrelated sources stays; the goals go above it, the forbidden words into `tools/names/build.py` beside the mechanical filters already there. The Giraffe's open question, herds' relief or hunters' curse, is answered on the page once, not per sitting.

**Art.** Magic's art description is five short fields (Colour, Location, Action, Focus, Mood), composition left to the artist. The page adds the behaviour the scene shows (the foals in the centre of the night circle; the crew eating behind the capo). One checkable fact per painting, never a history; the animal stays an animal.

**Character.** A legend is an individual of the society who keeps, breaks or embodies one line of the page: the Leopard is the herd's line "the young are kept in the middle" broken; the Giraffe is the lookout as an individual.

## 8. How this can be wrong, and the tests

The hypothesis is: a card with a society page behind it gets its name, art and character faster and the result survives the week; a card without one does not. The record so far (Ash with a legend, the herds without) is two data points and fits other stories (Ash is a famous animal with a strong stereotype to invert; the herds are three cards at once). Tests, cheapest first:

- **Regenerate.** Write the Pride's page from Theron, Adira, Leo and Lea, then give a cold agent only the page and a new lion card and ask for twenty names. If the list does not land in the register of the four gold names, the page does not hold the generator and the form is wrong before it is used on a failing card. (The understanding study's test: a generator derives the known results back out.)
- **The live case.** Write the herds' page, run the naming skill on the Giraffe with goals from it, count Martin's grabs on sight against the Oct 21 sitting, where none of the names was applied. One or two settles in a sitting is the skill's own bar.
- **The carry test.** Show a stranger a legendary's painting, name and line, and ask what its society lives by. If they cannot say the bargain in a sentence, the page stayed on the page (the Grimoire rule).
- **The kill condition.** If after three paged cards the grab rate is no better than the legends alone produced (Ash and Azamandulai each got a draft name the day the legend was written), the legend is the lever and the page is overhead; stop writing pages and keep writing legends.

## 9. Open, and not decided

- Whether Martin wants a society layer at all, or legends alone. The Oct 21 note is an agent's wording; no message of his on it is in the Mac's records.
- Which societies exist (section 5 is a reading), and the namer for each, above all for the herds and the City.
- Whether the game has a shared "what happened" or only the ground.
- Whether a mixed-species herd is one society or three; the sources have both.
- Whether the pages should be written by the agent and attacked by Martin, or dictated by Martin and formed by the agent; the Ash legend was the second and worked.
- What the sources never showed: a real one-page society sheet from any studio, the Magic or Riot handoff from world to name, Hearthstone's method, Warriors' internal lists. The form in section 6 is derived from cases, not copied from a document.

## Recommendation

Write the herds' page first, from documentary behaviour, with its three legendaries and the Leopard reading from it, since the herd is the live failure in names and the family whose playstyle is newest; put the Giraffe through the naming skill with goals from line 5 and judge the page by whether its effects, art and name come easier than they did on Oct 21.
