# Small-team creature worlds: how societies became mechanics

Scope and honesty about coverage. The primary sources I could fetch are the Leder Games blog diaries for the Marauder and Homeland expansions, the "Hard Cuts" post on Root's late development, Rain World interviews (Game Developer, Vice, DualShockers, a 2014 Q&A), Hollow Knight interviews (Brutal Gamer 2014, MCV, Game Informer, ACMI), Armello interviews, and a handful of card-game interviews. I could not retrieve Wehrle's original 2018 Root diaries on BoardGameGeek (blocked), the Riverfolk Kickstarter diary (blocked), or the Homo Ludens video on the factions' historical inspirations. So the base-game faction-by-faction derivation is largely absent here; what I have is the later diaries, which show the same method applied to new factions, and the post-Kickstarter rebuild of the Woodland Alliance. Where a line is my inference rather than the source's claim, it is marked "Inference".

## 1. Society to mechanic (Root above all)

**The method, as the later diaries show it.** Wehrle and his co-designers start from a social position (who holds power, how, and what that costs them) and ask what a player in that position would do each turn and what would hurt them. The Warlord diary is the clearest statement. The faction is defined against the existing cast: Root's earlier factions "have either broad governing authority (Marquise, Eyrie) or distributed bottom-up leadership (Lizards, Woodland Alliance)", so the new one is a charismatic single leader ([Marauder diary 2](https://ledergames.com/blogs/news/root-designer-diary-2-rats-let-s-get-rowdy), paraphrased by the fetch tool). Each mechanic then expresses one clause of that idea: a single Warlord pawn that other players can see and predict (the leader as focal point), three stats that cap how many actions he gets per phase (a commander who cannot control everything), a mood that must change every turn (a leader's whims), fire that spreads randomly and scores for destruction (willingness to devastate), and a diminished successor who can use moods but cannot recruit when the Warlord dies. The diary's own summary of the balance: "Everything is the careful dance on the edge of a knife as it should be."

Mechanism: the social description does two jobs. It generates the verbs (raid, burn, ransom, assemble) and it generates the constraint that makes the verbs costly (overextension, fragility). A faction that only had verbs would be a toolkit; a faction with the matching weakness reads as a character.

**Order of operations.** The sources I could read show: social idea, then a first mechanical pass that is deliberately straightforward, then testing, complexity growth, and a large cut by one designer. Josh Yearsley on the Homeland factions: start with a straightforward design, complexity grows as systems are tested, then he "makes large cuts and reconceptualizes what works so the pieces fit together more smoothly" ([Homeland diary 7](https://ledergames.com/blogs/news/root-the-homeland-expansion-designer-diary-7-third-print-and-play), paraphrase). Art follows: the Badgers began as Turtles and were renamed, "reportedly for art coherence" ([Marauder diary 3](https://ledergames.com/blogs/news/root-designer-diary-3-seeking-out-the-hidden-root-the-marauder-expansion)). Inference: the faction's name and look are cheap to change late; its mechanical shape is not, so the shape is the thing the society must fix first.

**Where a faction failed to read, and the fix.**

- The Woodland Alliance is the best-documented failure. After the Kickstarter, a trusted playtester told Wehrle the game was "missing its edge". The original Alliance was a Netrunner-style "conspiracies" heist system, which "didn't fit the scale or spirit of the game", and development had softened the faction until it was "getting worse" as the game got fairer. Wehrle deleted every conspiracy card and rebuilt it around sympathy tokens that spread across the map and a supporter stack, a second hand used to place sympathy and trigger revolts. Marching through sympathetic clearings feeds the stack, and removing sympathy triggers outrage. He called the result a "responsive, hydraulic system" ([Hard Cuts](https://ledergames.com/blogs/news/root-hard-cuts-the-continued-development-of-root)). Mechanism: an insurgency is a population that leans against an occupier; the fixed version makes the population a board object (tokens in clearings) and makes the occupier's own movement feed the insurgent. The failed version made the insurgent a heist crew, which is a different social shape. Wehrle names the root cause as a habit of minimizing components under publisher cost pressure: his old challenge was "seeing how much gameplay I could shove into a handful of cubes", and the design came together once he allowed new pieces.
- The Badgers (Stone Seekers) show a faction identity pulling against a mechanic. Patrick Leder's original brief was "a faction that was hard to kill and a faction that was slow to act." Early versions made the Badgers useful to others (shared buildings, roads) to give them scoring; Yearsley found it contradicted the role: "Why slam into their wall of teeth if you can benefit from it?" and "Semi-cooperation really just really wasn't working. So I cut it." ([Marauder diary 3](https://ledergames.com/blogs/news/root-designer-diary-3-seeking-out-the-hidden-root-the-marauder-expansion)). Lesson stated by the source: the incentive structure must agree with the social role, or the faction stops reading as what it is.
- The Warlord lost his three-personalities idea after about 15 minutes of testing, and an attempt to steal items by burning buildings was replaced by raiding because it punished factions that craft ([Marauder diary 2](https://ledergames.com/blogs/news/root-designer-diary-2-rats-let-s-get-rowdy)). The Knaves were cut from three Captains to one because testers found them "fun to play as, but not fun to play against" ([Homeland diary 7](https://ledergames.com/blogs/news/root-the-homeland-expansion-designer-diary-7-third-print-and-play)). The Twilight Council's first assembly was a card game testers called "bat poker"; it was replaced by an auction because balancing it across every faction needed too many rules and its secret planning "gave no shared story hooks" (same source, paraphrase). Mechanism: a rule that creates no event other players can see and talk about fails to deliver the society.
- Criticism of the result: the Lizard Cult is called out as having "major balance issues" in one review, and the same review calls the game "border-line inaccessible to new players", with three manuals ([Hi Adventure review](https://hiadventurecast.com/root-review/), a third-party reviewer, not the designers). Wehrle himself concedes in the Homeland diary that the Riverfolk factions' politics are hard for newer players, "especially the Lizard Cult", and says he structured the Homeland politics "to ensure they are more accessible" ([Homeland diary 1](https://ledergames.com/blogs/news/root-the-homeland-expansion-design-diary-1-coming-to-kickstarter-october-22-2024), paraphrase). Inference: the more a society's shape depends on negotiation with other players, the more it demands a player who already reads the world.

## 2. How much was written

- **Root.** The diaries show no standalone bible. What existed was the faction idea itself, which a BGG commenter says documents "a particular ideological idea" per faction (second-hand, [BGG thread](https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/2038326/sudden-inspiration-about-the-backstory-of-root); I could not fetch it). For the Marauder expansion the first diary is titled "backstory" and the factions are defined by one sentence each (the Warlord leads a horde recently arrived; the Stone Seekers are outsiders recovering relics) ([diary 1](https://ledergames.com/blogs/news/root-the-marauder-expansion-designer-diary-1-backstory)). Wehrle's stated working method is that he "can workshop designs for months only to throw them out" and uses Python to "game out game states to bend towards a desired thematic evocation" ([Game Data podcast page](https://www.gamedatapodcast.com/p/cole-wehrle-design-arcs-root-oath-john-company-pax-pamir), paraphrase of the episode blurb). Inference: the working document is a one-line social idea plus a running design, not prose lore.
- **Hollow Knight.** The team of four (Pellen, Gibson, technical director Dave Kazi, composer Chris Larkin) built from game-jam prototypes ([MCV](https://mcvuk.com/development-news/when-we-made-hollow-knight/)). In 2014 they said they planned about six large districts with side areas, and "produced one large chart of Hallownest" ([Brutal Gamer](https://brutalgamer.com/2014/12/01/hollow-knight-interview-with-indie-developer-team-cherrys-william-pellen-and-ari-gibson/)). A fan post says a 2016 interview "briefly mention[s] their lore document" ([post](https://x.com/mossbag69/status/1422177488457830403)); I could not read it, and its contents are unknown. Their 2025 account of Silksong: foundations are set first, then "the world then suggests what else should exist"; Pharloom is "an ecosystem, not a series of levels", and planning is monthly by area but kept flexible ([ACMI](https://www.acmi.net.au/stories-and-ideas/from-ludum-dare-to-pharloom/), paraphrase). Gibson's advice: "A world can just mean a cast of interesting characters in a small setting" ([MCV](https://mcvuk.com/development-news/when-we-made-hollow-knight/)). Inference: a map, a chart and character sketches, with the society emerging from the places.
- **Rain World.** Jakobsson says "the world of Rain World wasn't really invented back then", meaning at the prototype stage ([2014 Q&A](https://indiegameenthusiast.blogspot.com/2014/01/q-joar-jakobsson-on-rain-world.html)). What existed was a prototype of three creatures (bat, slugcat that eats the bat, lizard that eats the slugcat) and a feeling ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/crafting-the-complex-chaotic-ecosystem-of-i-rain-world-i-)). Therrien says the prototype's vision shaped the project for years ([IGF interview](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/road-to-the-igf-videocult-s-i-rain-world-i-)).
- **Flesh and Blood.** The only team in the set that wrote a world first. White says it took seven years because they needed "a world and story arc to integrate into the gameplay and the products", with "enough design space to elegantly evolve over time" ([Star City Games](https://articles.starcitygames.com/flesh-and-blood/creator-of-flesh-and-blood-discusses-games-latest-milestone-upcoming-world-championship/), partly paraphrase). A commenter reports White discussing classes and heroes "as pillars" ([Rathe Times comment](https://rathetimes.com/articles/an-interview-with-james-white-of-legend-story-studios), second-hand). I did not find what the world document contained.
- **Armello.** Eight months designing on paper, then about a year as a pen-and-paper prototype ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/road-to-the-igf-league-of-geeks-i-armello-i-)). The world is a short premise: a stranger-king united the clans, then the Rot corrupted him ([Gaming Furever](https://gamingfurever.com/editorials/interview-with-armello-director-and-league-of-geeks-founder-trent-kusters)).

None of these sources reports what any designer would write down now. That question is open.

## 3. The player's reading

There is little direct evidence (no surveys); what I have is designer statements and reviews, so most of this is inference.

- **Verbs carry factions.** The Hi Adventure review reads the factions entirely off what they do: cats expand and build, the Alliance recruits sympathizers and revolts, Eyrie are "scrupulous and bureaucratic", otters sell services to all, lizards recruit "downtrodden" outcasts. "Every minor detail of this game is used for its theme" ([review](https://hiadventurecast.com/root-review/)). The same reviewer notes the Eyrie are "not especially distinct morally from the cats", so a society whose verbs resemble another's blurs. Hirelings were colour-matched to their factions so the world feels less empty without new rules ([hireling diary](https://ledergames.com/blogs/news/root-the-marauder-expansion-designer-diary-4-hirelings-galore)).
- **Rain World teaches by behaviour.** Creatures "exist there for themselves"; lizards "can learn to recognize you", a vulture "may decide to track you specifically", and a scavenger you save from a vulture may become a friend, "eventually even gain the trust of the entire pack" ([DualShockers](https://www.dualshockers.com/rain-world-gets-new-details-unique-living-ecosystem-protagonist-slugcat/)). The player learns the scavengers as a society (tolls, reputation, pack loyalty) from encounters. The lore is deliberately out of reach: Jakobsson wants the player to be like his Manhattan rat, who knows the tracks but "doesn't understand why humans built the station" ([Vice](https://www.vice.com/en/article/rain-world-is-like-stalker-but-a-platformer-and-youre-a-rodent/)). The cost: designing for self-preservation can be "super difficult and frustrating", and one streamer got "no-win situations" while another had a clear path ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/crafting-the-complex-chaotic-ecosystem-of-i-rain-world-i-)). A fan thread says the ancient civilization is never named, which is a fan observation, not designer intent.
- **Hollow Knight teaches by place and cast.** Game Informer: narrative details are "planted subtly throughout the world", signs "in areas that provide appropriate context", and the shade, a simple death mechanic, ended up "having a huge impact on the lore" ([Game Informer](https://gameinformer.com/2018/10/15/the-making-of-hollow-knight)). Characters are remembered by personality (a stressed cartographer, a grumpy Zote) more than plot ([Brutal Gamer](https://brutalgamer.com/2014/12/01/hollow-knight-interview-with-indie-developer-team-cherrys-william-pellen-and-ari-gibson/)). Early Kickstarter updates were written as in-world bestiary entries (for example "An overgrown bloodsucker that will patiently stalk its prey until it finds the right time to strike"), so the creature's society-role is in the voice from the start ([ACMI](https://www.acmi.net.au/stories-and-ideas/from-ludum-dare-to-pharloom/)).
- **What went unread** is not documented anywhere I could fetch. Inference: one reviewer needed a how-to video after reading three manuals, yet described every faction by its verbs; the verbs carried and the text did not.
- **Armello's clans read by role.** The tutorial ties each clan to a mechanical focus (wolves combat, rats trickery, rabbits magic, bears fighting the Rot), and a reviewer praises the "distinct clan themes and playstyles" while finding some heroes "a bit too specialized" ([From Galaxies to Dungeons](https://fromgalaxiestodungeons.wordpress.com/2016/06/27/design-overview-of-armello/)).

## 4. Names

I found little on naming conventions; none of the sources states one.

- Root faction names are role plus animal (Marquise de Cat, Eyrie Dynasties, Woodland Alliance, Riverfolk Company, Lizard Cult, Corvid Conspiracy, Underground Duchy, Keepers in Iron, Vagabond). The name states the political form: Marquise (aristocracy), Dynasties, Alliance, Company, Cult, Conspiracy, Duchy. This is my reading of the list in the [Hi Adventure review](https://hiadventurecast.com/root-review/) and the [Homeland diary 1](https://ledergames.com/blogs/news/root-the-homeland-expansion-design-diary-1-coming-to-kickstarter-october-22-2024) (Lilypad Diaspora, Twilight Council). Inference: the political noun does the society-reading work; the animal is the species fact. Renamings in the sources: Turtles to Badgers, and "Seekers" not always the name ([diary 3](https://ledergames.com/blogs/news/root-designer-diary-3-seeking-out-the-hidden-root-the-marauder-expansion)).
- Armello: clans are plain animal names (Rat, Rabbit, Bear, Wolf). Kusters: "there are instinctual archetypes and personalities humans place upon different animals", and the team limited species to European animals as "imperative to the verisimilitude of our game and universe" ([Gaming Furever](https://gamingfurever.com/editorials/interview-with-armello-director-and-league-of-geeks-founder-trent-kusters)). Later clans (Bandit, Dragon) broke the European rule; the Dragon heroes include a Komodo dragon ([PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/armello/armello-dlc-bandit-clan), [store listing](https://store.playstation.com/en-us/product/UP1120-CUSA03300_00-ARMELLDRAGONSCEA)).
- Hollow Knight: the protagonist is nameless; Hornet's lineage (Pale King and Herrah the Beast) is on [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hornet_(Hollow_Knight)) only as a search snippet I did not read in full. No interview I fetched explains the naming. Rain World: Slugcat is a species-role name; I found no naming statement.
- Sorcery: names, art, type line and rules text should align so "a player can guess a card's function from its name and art"; some cards start from the effect and the name comes after (Polar Bears were built to close distance, "their name and flavor came afterward") ([Sorcery interview](https://sorcerytcg.com/news/interview-with-game-creator-erik-olofsson), paraphrase).

## 5. Real animals versus folk

- **Rain World (animals as animals).** Buys: plausibility needs no explanation, and societies are inferred from territory and appetite. Creatures use "room attractiveness" (a creature prefers rooms with water) and can leave when they sense food elsewhere, so territory is a tendency, not a script ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/crafting-the-complex-chaotic-ecosystem-of-i-rain-world-i-)). Jakobsson chose "few, well made creatures rather than many species" and says the trio will "always be the core" ([Q&A](https://indiegameenthusiast.blogspot.com/2014/01/q-joar-jakobsson-on-rain-world.html)). Costs: the society is thin (the scavengers are the exception) and the designers cannot script, so they shape levels by changing a wall by one tile. Therrien describes lizards and vultures as "your brothers and sisters, struggling to survive as you are" ([IGF](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/road-to-the-igf-videocult-s-i-rain-world-i-)). The one human-made element is the abandoned machinery, read by the animals as incomprehensible.
- **Root (animals as people).** Buys: instant faction identity from the species plus the political noun, and humans can map real politics onto it. Costs: the Homeland diaries concede a population problem: "a fox clearing is one where the foxes have overriding influence", meaning control does not equal population ([Homeland diary 1](https://ledergames.com/blogs/news/root-the-homeland-expansion-design-diary-1-coming-to-kickstarter-october-22-2024)). Root's wildlife is not plausible behaviour; plausibility is political, not biological.
- **Hollow Knight.** Bugs chosen for production: "Bugs are simple to draw so they're good when you want to produce a lot of characters quickly" ([Brutal Gamer](https://brutalgamer.com/2014/12/01/hollow-knight-interview-with-indie-developer-team-cherrys-william-pellen-and-ari-gibson/)). The first creature texts keep animal habit in the voice ("patiently stalk its prey"). Inference: the species supplies appetite; the rank and caste layer is applied on top.
- **Mouse Guard.** The cleanest place where species behaviour is the rule. Nature is defined as "for mice it's in their nature to escape, hide, climb and forage"; a high Nature means "the more mouse-like you are", and Guard service is "un-mouse-like", so using Nature for Guard work costs something ([review](https://islaythedragon.com/featured/its-what-you-fight-for-a-review-of-mouse-guard-roleplaying-game/)). The institution is also a rule: the matriarch dispatches patrols, the Guard has no authority in towns, and Winter sessions are for changing beliefs and promotions. Mechanism: the tension between what the species does and what the role asks is the stat.
- **Armello.** Limits anthropomorphism on purpose ("restraint... imperative to the verisimilitude"), keeping archetype shortcuts from the species while giving clans an institutional job ([Gaming Furever](https://gamingfurever.com/editorials/interview-with-armello-director-and-league-of-geeks-founder-trent-kusters)).

## 6. Adding later

- **Root.** Wehrle thought "6 factions was an absolute limit" originally; the Marauder expansion changed setup to a draft and hirelings to allow more, and its stated goals were two-player factions, a setup system that eases expansion, and minor factions ([Marauder diary 1](https://ledergames.com/blogs/news/root-the-marauder-expansion-designer-diary-1-backstory), paraphrase). Each new faction is defined by contrast with the existing ones (Warlord against distributed and governing powers; Knaves as "reconceptualize and expand on the Vagabond"; the Homeland pair as the Woodland's own internal politics, which is a new axis rather than another occupier) ([Homeland diary 5](https://ledergames.com/blogs/news/root-the-homeland-expansion-design-diary-5-knavish-behavior), [diary 1](https://ledergames.com/blogs/news/root-the-homeland-expansion-design-diary-1-coming-to-kickstarter-october-22-2024)). The Knaves needed "the New Thing" beyond "more of the same". Inference: the first world had to contain a space where societies relate (the clearings and the Woodland population) so that a new society has something to push against.
- **Armello.** The first world was four animal clans and a king; DLC added Bandit and Dragon clans with their own quest systems, dice and a pilgrimage story ([PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/armello/armello-dlc-bandit-clan)). The existing premise (clans competing for the throne) absorbed new clans without edits; a Komodo dragon needed an explicit "faith" reason to exist.
- **Hollow Knight.** Silksong began as DLC and became a new world because Hornet's movement did not suit Hallownest; the team built Pharloom around her, with the Citadel's influence felt across the map ([ACMI](https://www.acmi.net.au/stories-and-ideas/from-ludum-dare-to-pharloom/)).
- **Rain World.** New creatures are meant to be rarer, "placed in special locations on the world map", so the core trio stays intact ([Q&A](https://indiegameenthusiast.blogspot.com/2014/01/q-joar-jakobsson-on-rain-world.html)).
- **Flesh and Blood.** The seven-year delay bought "design space to elegantly evolve over time" ([SCG](https://articles.starcitygames.com/flesh-and-blood/creator-of-flesh-and-blood-discusses-games-latest-milestone-upcoming-world-championship/)).

## What transfers to Animal Kingdom and what does not

- Transfers: write each society as a social position stated against the others, then derive verbs and the matching weakness. Root's Warlord shows the pair (focal point plus overextension) is what reads. Reason: the weakness is what turns a toolkit into a character.
- Transfers: put the society's verb in the deck's mechanic and check that two societies do not share a verb (Eyrie read as the cats' moral twin). Reason: a blurred verb blurs the society.
- Transfers: keep the page short and spend it on contrast with neighbours, not description. Reason: none of these teams worked from long prose, and the one that did write a world (Flesh and Blood) took seven years.
- Transfers: Mouse Guard's Nature stat, where the species' habit and the role pull against each other, is the best model for real animals with roles. Reason: it makes plausibility a cost, which fits "animals, not folk in clothes". Rain World shows the other half: tendencies, not scripts.
- Transfers: a society's name should carry its political shape (Root's role noun). For real animals the species name is already the plain name, so the role goes in the individual (legendaries) and in the archetype label, not the species.
- Transfers: Rain World's rule that creatures exist for themselves, with the player as a small inhabitant, matches "told from the animals' point of view". Reason: it gives reasons for animals to be where they are without humans.
- Does not transfer: Root's political vocabulary (Marquise, Company, Cult) assumes folk who hold offices. Animals as animals cannot be a Duchy. Reason: that is the folk-in-clothes choice Animal Kingdom rejects.
- Does not transfer: Hollow Knight's and Rain World's reliance on exploration to deliver the world. A two-player card game has no map walk; the signals that remain are names, art, the repeated verb and a flavour line. Reason: the delivery channel is different, even if the lesson (verbs and places over text) holds.
- Does not transfer: Root's rebuild after playtester feedback and Rain World's level tweaks assume long iteration with real players. Reason: Martin's playtesting is his own; the derivation has to be right before cards exist, which is the point of writing the pages.
- Caution: Root's Lizard Cult shows a society that is clever but needs the player to know the world. Reason: a faction that depends on reading other players or lore is the one that reads worst.
