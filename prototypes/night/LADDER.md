# Skill ladder

How far each alive prototype's skill ceiling sits, by proxy: a ladder of bots, Random < Greedy < Expert N < Expert 4N < Expert 16N, where N is the Expert budget (play-outs or iterations) its builder gated with; 16N drops to 8N where it would not finish in time (03, 13, 15). One rung below the ladder, Expert N against Expert N/4, shows whether N already sits past the point where search stops paying. Each pair plays 200 games, every deal twice with the seats swapped; a cell is the stronger side's score in percent (wins plus half the draws) with its 95% interval. "Top > Random" is the top rung over 100 games where the gate's Expert N scored under 99% against Random, otherwise the gate's own Expert N number. "Mirror draws" is the draw rate of the gate's Expert N mirror. Each folder's `ladder.json` has every count; `ladder.mjs` reruns it.

A clear win means the interval sits above 50. Near 50 means more search buys nothing: either the game has little left to find (big gaps, Expert against Random or Greedy, still win big: "shallow") or luck decides (even big gaps win modestly). The ladder measures each prototype's own search, which samples play-outs under a fixed play-out rule, so "shallow" means shallow for that search; a stronger kind of search could still find more.

41 is one player against the game: every rung plays the same 200 seeded games and a pair is compared game by game (a win beats a loss, then more Wall, then more rounds); its "Mirror draws" is the share of even pairs at the top. In 43 a rung's reroll judgement scales with k² rather than k, so its rungs above N search more than their names say.
