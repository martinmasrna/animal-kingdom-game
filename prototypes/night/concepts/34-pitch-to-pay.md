# 34 Pitch to pay

Every number here is a placeholder. Borrowed: Flesh and Blood's pitching, where a card spent for resources gives its pitch value and goes to the bottom of the deck, and the hand refills at the end of each turn. The rest is not that game: a unit board in the usual attack style, no heroes, no blocking from hand.

**The question.** When every card is both a unit and money, is choosing which cards to burn for mana a real decision every turn?

## Setup

- Each player has the same 30-card deck, shuffled. Life 20. Board max 6 units. There is no mana ramp.
- Random first player; the first player starts with 4 cards, the second with 5.

## Turn

1. Your units become ready. From your 15th turn on, you first lose 1 life, then 2 on your 16th, and so on.
2. **Pay by pitching.** To play a card, pitch cards from your hand: each gives its Pitch value in mana for this turn. Pitched cards go to the bottom of your deck in an order you choose. Mana not spent by the end of the turn is lost.
3. Play cards. A unit cannot attack on the turn it is played.
4. Each ready unit may attack once: an enemy unit (both deal their attack to each other; damage stays) or the enemy player.
5. End of turn: draw up to 5 cards. When your deck is empty, shuffle your discard pile into it; with both empty, draw nothing.

Destroyed units and used spells go to their owner's discard pile.

## End

Life 0 or less loses; both at once is a draw.

## Builder notes

- Greedy: plays the most expensive card it can pay for, pitching its highest-pitch cards first, and repeats; attacks that destroy a unit and survive first, then the enemy player.
- Racer: every attack and every Hit at the enemy player.
- Set positions: lethal (the opponent on 5 life, Hit 5 and two Pips in hand: pitch both Pips and play Hit 5); don't overpay (a 2-cost card and a Pip in hand: pitch the Pip alone, not a second card as well); free trade (your ready Line against an enemy Raider: attack it).
