# Night run concepts

55 concepts generated, 40 alive, 15 dead on the paper criteria. A dead concept's number names the criterion that killed it: (1) no cost dimension, (2) a dominant strategy found by reasoning, (3) solitaire, (4) no end, (5) too heavy for its depth. Nothing was cut for looking unpromising. Where two ideas would have shared one loop, only one was written up, so every number below is a different loop.

Where a concept takes a mechanism from a real game, the line names the game; each of those was checked against its rules on the web before use. The specs use functional names only.

## Concepts

- **01 Score-or-fight duel.** The brief's fixed entry: units with attack and health, each turn a ready unit attacks an enemy unit or scores its current health, first to 40. **Alive.**
- **02 Attack and block.** Life duel where the defender chooses blockers, attackers stay exhausted through the opponent's turn and damage heals at end of turn (Magic's combat). **Alive.**
- **03 Exert to score.** Points race to 20 where a unit scores by exerting and only exerted units can be attacked, so scoring exposes it (Lorcana's quest and challenge rule). **Alive.**
- **04 Lanes and scale.** Four lanes; at the end of your turn each unit hits the unit opposite it or, if the lane is empty, tips a shared scale; five ahead wins (Inscryption's scale). **Alive.**
- **05 Front and back rows.** The front row shields the back row and your life; ranged units chip the enemy front without being hit back. **Alive.**
- **06 Breaking sites.** Three shared sites; a site scores the moment total power on it reaches its threshold, the side with more power taking the big share (Smash Up's breakpoints). **Alive.**
- **07 Blind fronts.** Players alternately commit cards face-down to three fronts worth 1, 2 and 3, energy banks between rounds, then everything is revealed and majorities score. **Alive.**
- **08 Formation flags.** One card a turn onto nine flags; the better three-card formation claims a flag (Battle Line). **Dead (1):** every turn is one card and no card costs more than another; numbers set worth, nothing sets price.
- **09 Momentum rope.** No units: cards shove a shared marker now or add velocity that keeps moving it every turn; reach the far end. **Alive.**
- **10 Engine war.** Market deckbuilder with a row of five (Star Realms' trade row) where points come only from Posts on the table, which the opponent shoots. **Alive.**
- **11 Victory-card race.** Buy coin and victory cards into your own deck; attack cards slow the opponent's buying. **Dead (3):** points sit inside the buyer's own deck where nothing reaches them; attacks only delay future buys, so the race is two solitaires.
- **12 Deck is life.** No life total: damage burns cards off the top of your deck, drawing extra costs life, recycling your discard heals, and you lose when you cannot draw. **Alive.**
- **13 Open discards.** Discard piles are face-up and the opponent may play any card from yours for its cost plus one, so every card you use becomes theirs to use. **Alive.**
- **14 Response duel.** No board: spells hit the opponent, and mana left at the end of your turn pays for one response (cancel, ward, reflect) during theirs. **Alive.**
- **15 Key pieces.** Win by holding three different Keys in play at the start of your turn; Keys are bodies the opponent can attack, Guards protect them. **Alive.**
- **16 Showdown.** Each round both buy a face-down hand with mana, the mana each spent is shown, then poker-style betting with chips that are your life. **Alive.**
- **17 Prize auction.** Sealed bids from a hand of coin cards for one prize a round; prizes are better coin or points that clog the deck; the loser keeps its bid. **Alive.**
- **18 Edge flip grid.** 4×4 grid; cards with four edge numbers are priced by their strength, and a placed card flips weaker adjacent enemy cards; most cells when full (Triple Triad's flip). **Alive.**
- **19 Supply grid.** 5×5 grid; you place only next to a chain connected to your Base, strikes compare power, cut-off pieces die; destroy the Base or own more cells. **Alive.**
- **20 Chain combo.** Every card played this turn raises a chain, and finishers deal damage times the chain. **Dead (3):** the whole engine runs inside one turn the opponent cannot touch; their progress is unreachable and racing beats interacting.
- **21 Round purse.** Best of three rounds of total power, a round ends when both pass (Gwent's structure), all paid from one mana purse for the whole match. **Alive.**
- **22 Possession.** A ball on nine zones; each play the attacker and defender pick cards at once from a rock-paper-scissors table priced in stamina; most goals in 20 plays. **Alive.**
- **23 Count and bases.** Baseball: pitch and swing cards picked at once, with balls, strikes, outs, bases and innings. **Dead (5):** five bookkeeping systems around one repeated guess; the rules cost far more to learn than the decisions they add.
- **24 Rally.** Tennis points: a return needs reach at least the incoming shot's pace, every shot costs stamina, first to three games. **Alive.**
- **25 Range duel.** Two fighters on a seven-space line pick moves at once; speed decides who acts first, range whether it hits, energy pays. **Alive.**
- **26 Exhaustion race.** Race on a track playing one movement card a turn; the leader takes exhaustion cards, followers slipstream (Flamme Rouge). **Dead (1):** every turn is exactly one card, and a big card's only price is leaving the deck, the same for every card.
- **27 Bust duel.** Both draw from their own decks into lines side by side, stop or push, bust on strain; the higher line hits the other, then each buys with its line's power. **Alive.**
- **28 Curling line.** Stones on a one-dimensional sheet push the first stone in their path; closest to the mark scores, the side that scored throws first next end (curling's scoring); precise throws cost energy. **Alive.**
- **29 Hidden runner.** Asymmetric: the Runner moves secretly on a 4×4 grid, announcing only the card type and showing itself every third round, while two Hunters search and corner it (Scotland Yard's hidden movement); both sides play costed move decks. **Alive.**
- **30 Siege clock.** Asymmetric decks: the Attacker must break a gate of 20 within ten rounds; the Defender blocks, shoots and repairs. **Alive.**
- **31 Claims.** Play any card face-down naming any card on the shared list and pay the named cost; the opponent may challenge; a caught lie or a failed challenge costs 3 life (Coup's challenge). **Alive.**
- **32 Hidden contracts.** Unit board with no life; points come from two face-down contracts whose conditions you meet and the opponent must guess and deny; first to 10. **Alive.**
- **33 Time track.** No turns: whoever is behind on the time track acts next (Patchwork's rule); cards and attacks cost time, units recover on the same timeline. **Alive.**
- **34 Pitch to pay.** No mana: you pay by pitching cards from hand for their pitch value, pitched cards go under the deck and the hand refills to five (Flesh and Blood's pitch), on a unit board. **Alive.**
- **35 Shared count.** Running total to 31 scoring for 15, 31 and the last card (cribbage pegging), played from built decks. **Dead (1):** one card a play, and a card's only cost is its own value on a count that limits both players alike; no card can be priced against what it does.
- **36 Shared well.** Both players take mana from one pool that refills each turn; what you leave, the opponent gets. **Dead (2):** leaving mana only feeds the opponent, so spending all of it every turn is always right, and the pool collapses into a flat income.
- **37 Price tracks.** Goods in your deck sell at shared prices that fall when sold and rise each round; cards push or crash prices; guess what the opponent holds. **Alive.**
- **38 Costed tricks.** Trick-taking with follow-suit and trumps from built decks, where cards cost energy by rank and each trick's prize is shown in advance. **Alive.**
- **39 Commander grid.** 5×6 grid; units deploy next to your Commander, move and strike; the Commander fights too; kill theirs. **Alive.**
- **40 Simultaneous turns.** Both plan the whole turn at once (plays and attack targets), then everything resolves together. **Alive.**
- **41 Wave defense.** One player against the game's threat deck walking down three lanes, with defenders and spells from a deck you add to every four rounds. **Alive.**
- **42 Real-time lane.** Mana flows by the second and an eight-card deck cycles through a hand of four (Clash Royale's cycle); units walk one lane and fight for three minutes. **Alive.**
- **43 Auto deck battle.** Buy cards from a shop with gold each round; battles resolve themselves, the top cards of both shuffled decks fighting front to front until a deck runs out (the front-unit autobattle of Super Auto Pets). **Alive.**
- **44 Bump placement.** Five shared action spaces; players alternately place costed cards, a stronger card bumps a weaker one back to its owner's hand, spaces pay at round end. **Alive.**
- **45 Swing neutrals.** Three shared Neutral pieces with loyalty tracks; influence cards pull them, and each Neutral leaning your way hits the opponent at the end of your turn. **Alive.**
- **46 Echo mana.** Your mana each turn is what the opponent spent on their last turn plus one; spending little starves them. **Alive.**
- **47 Countdown pile.** A shared pile of 30; cards take amounts off it; whoever empties it exactly wins. **Dead (2):** leaving the pile inside the opponent's reach loses, so both keep it out of reach and it never falls; forced down, it reduces to "finish if you can, otherwise leave it just above their reach".
- **48 Junk war.** Market deckbuilder where points are scored by playing cards and the main attack is pushing junk into the opponent's deck; cleaning cards remove it. **Alive.**
- **49 Stacked deck.** No shuffle: you order your deck before the match and draw from the top. **Dead (2):** with a mana curve, ordering the deck by cost is always right; the build decision has one answer.
- **50 Split offers.** Reveal cards, split them into two piles, the opponent takes one and you the other; majorities score. **Dead (1):** cards carry no cost; their price is only what the split makes it.
- **51 Discount engine.** Buy from an open market into a tableau where each card permanently cheapens later buys of its colour; race to points. **Dead (3):** the tableau is permanent and unreachable; the opponent can only race or take a card first.
- **52 Score-target solitaire.** Play poker-style hands from your deck to beat rising score targets, adding cards between rounds. **Dead (3):** the target never acts; nothing anyone does reaches the player's progress.
- **53 Passing move cards.** Pieces on a small board; each turn you use one of two move cards, which then passes to the opponent (Onitama's card pass). **Dead (1):** every turn is one move with one card; cards differ in what they allow, never in what they cost.
- **54 Crowd count.** Units go to three zones; at round end each zone goes to whoever has more units there. **Dead (2):** counting bodies means the cheapest bodies win every zone.
- **55 Shedding race.** Play a card matching the top card by suit or number, first to empty the hand wins, with built decks. **Dead (1):** one card a turn, and nothing costs more than anything else.

## For the builders

- Every spec is in `concepts/<nn>-<slug>.md`; all its numbers are placeholders, stated once at its top. Card and piece names are functional; keep them that way on the page.
- Each spec suggests the Greedy and Racer rules and set positions for `sanity.mjs`; they are suggestions, the brief's gate is the rule.
- Some loops need the gate mapped, and the spec says how: games with no turn order (17, 25, 40, 42, 43) report seat A's win rate in place of the first player's, and 22 reads kickoff possession; asymmetric games (29, 30) hold the role win rate to 30–70%; the solo game (41) compares Expert with each trivial bot on the same seeded games.
- The template's own record is worth knowing before building a unit duel: its Expert beat Greedy only 68% on vanilla Hearthstone rules, so on unit duels the 70% line needs the Expert to search for real.
